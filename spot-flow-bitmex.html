Executive Summary

Unlike perpetual derivatives — where DLT can act as a pure broker routing orders directly to BitMEX — EU spot crypto activity is constrained by MiFID II–style best execution obligations and emerging MiCA conduct rules. DLT cannot legally route 100% of EU client spot flow to a single venue without a structurally defensible framework.

This memorandum sets out the two viable models — Option 1: Riskless Principal / Financial Commission Broker and Option 2: Proprietary Trader / Market Maker — including their order flow mechanics, regulatory anchoring, and the practical implications for the share of flow that lands on BitMEX Global. Both models are designed so that the substantive majority of executed volume reaches the BitMEX order book.

Context

Why two models exist

The constraint that shapes both options

For perpetual derivatives, DLT operates under an introducing-broker / order-routing model: the order originates with the user, DLT relays it, and BitMEX is the execution venue and counterparty. EU best execution rules apply but are easily satisfied because a derivatives venue is the natural execution point.

For spot crypto, the legal categorisation under MiFID II / MiCA and most national regimes splits into two paths:

  • ▸ Reception & transmission of orders / execution on behalf of clients — the broker must demonstrate best execution and must not systematically prefer one venue without justification.
  • ▸ Dealing on own account — the broker is the counterparty to the client trade and operates under fair-price / market-making rules instead of multi-venue best execution.
Key constraint

Routing 100% of EU client spot flow to a single non-EU venue while presenting as a broker is the pattern regulators flag as circumvention. Both options below are designed to avoid that surface while still delivering the majority of executed volume to BitMEX.

Option 1

Riskless Principal / Financial Commission Broker

DLT executes against an aggregated book of BitMEX + Ouinex; Ouinex back-routes its flow into BitMEX.

Order flow — step by step

1
BitMEX and Ouinex stream L2 order books to DLT
Ouinex's book is itself constructed by quoting against the BitMEX book on its side, generally one tick tighter due to Ouinex's lower internal fee structure.
2
DLT publishes a single aggregated order book to the user
Predominantly BitMEX liquidity, with a thin layer of price-improved Ouinex levels. To the user the surface looks like one consolidated book.
3
User submits a marketable order to DLT
DLT receives the order under its broker license; the contractual relationship is broker-client, not principal-counterparty.
4
Smart Order Router selects best-priced levels
In normal book conditions ~75% of executed volume hits the BitMEX side, ~25% hits Ouinex's top-of-book. The split is the natural consequence of price priority, not a manual allocation.
5
Ouinex forwards 100% of its received flow to BitMEX Global
From BitMEX's perspective the entire flow ultimately lands in the BitMEX order book — either directly (75%) or via Ouinex (25%).

Advantages

  • Clean fit under the broker / best-execution paradigm
  • 100% of executed volume ultimately reaches the BitMEX order book
  • No proprietary balance-sheet risk for DLT
  • Operationally simple — no internalisation, no risk warehousing
  • Lower capital requirements (no own-account dealing license needed)

Disadvantages / Risks

  • Aggregated book is harder to display cleanly on the front end
  • Smart router and venue-selection logic must be built and continuously evidenced
  • Best-execution reporting (RTS 28-style) becomes mandatory
  • Regulators may still characterise the Ouinex hop as circumvention if Ouinex adds no genuine price improvement
  • Ouinex must remain a credible independent venue, not a captive entity
DLT note · Defensibility test

The structure stands up to scrutiny only if Ouinex demonstrably improves the price on the levels it contributes. If Ouinex levels are just a mirror of BitMEX with no improvement, the routing logic looks contrived. Recommendation: ensure Ouinex's published top-of-book is, on average, at least one tick inside the BitMEX best bid/ask on the symbols DLT supports.

Option 2

Proprietary Trader / Market Maker

DLT is the direct counterparty to client trades and hedges aggregate exposure into BitMEX in threshold-sized blocks.

Order flow — step by step

1
DLT consumes the BitMEX Global order book as its price reference
DLT republishes a "DLT order book" sourced from BitMEX. To the user the displayed prices are those of BitMEX Global, with DLT as the named counterparty.
2
User submits an order, e.g. €100 to buy 0.001 BTC
The contractual relationship is principal-to-principal. DLT is the legal counterparty.
3
DLT executes the order on its own book at the BitMEX-referenced price
No hedge order is sent to BitMEX at this point. DLT carries the open position on its balance sheet.
4
DLT accumulates net exposure across all client flow
Multiple users' orders net into a single position. A risk threshold is defined per symbol (e.g. 0.01 BTC, configurable).
5
On crossing the threshold, DLT hedges into BitMEX in one block
A small residual (e.g. ~10%) may be warehoused intentionally — this is the visible evidence of "real" proprietary risk-taking, distinguishing DLT from a pure pass-through.

Advantages

  • User-facing UI can mirror the BitMEX global order book directly
  • Flow is fully internalised between DLT and BitMEX — no third venue required
  • Cleaner narrative: one venue, one liquidity source, one counterparty for the user
  • BitMEX captures all hedge flow as natural block trades
  • Fee economics simpler to model (no inter-venue rebates to track)

Disadvantages / Risks

  • DLT must hold an own-account dealing / market-making license
  • Higher regulatory capital (own-funds requirements under IFR/IFD)
  • Hedge orders are not deterministic — sometimes minutes of latency between client trade and BitMEX hedge
  • Still requires evidencing that quoted prices are "fair market" against multiple references
  • Conflict-of-interest policy and disclosures to clients mandatory
  • Could still be characterised as circumvention if the warehoused portion is negligible
Coinbase precedent · Read carefully

Coinbase's original EU spot model — act as MM under a prop trading license while hedging 100% to the global venue — was challenged precisely because the EU entity was treated as a façade with no real risk-taking. The current model (an EU entity that warehouses ~10% of exposure and routes the rest back) is what gives the structure substance.

For DLT, the warehoused percentage is therefore not just a risk parameter — it is the regulatory feature that defines Option 2 as dealing on own account rather than dressed-up brokerage. Recommendation: define the warehoused band explicitly in DLT's risk policy and document it as a fixed operating principle.

Additional considerations

Points worth surfacing with BitMEX

Pre-trade transparency & client disclosure

In both options, EU clients must receive an order-execution policy describing venue selection and price formation. In Option 2 specifically, the prospectus / terms must disclose that DLT acts as principal and that BitMEX is the underlying price source.

Reporting & surveillance

Trade reporting obligations (MiFIR / MiCAR equivalent) sit with DLT, not BitMEX. Market-abuse surveillance under MAR also remains with DLT for client-facing activity. BitMEX's own surveillance covers the order book it operates.

Settlement & custody

Client custody is independent of execution model. Both options assume BitMEX as execution venue; settlement/custody arrangements (omnibus vs segregated, on-chain vs internal book transfer) should be defined separately in the integration spec.

Client classification

Retail clients carry stricter obligations under both models (appropriateness assessment, costs & charges disclosure, suitability for advised flow). Professional and ECPs allow more streamlined execution. Indicate which client category DLT primarily intends to onboard via the BitMEX flow.

Hybrid model (worth mentioning)

A third route is operationally possible: Option 1 as the default for retail flow, Option 2 for professional clients who explicitly accept principal execution. This is more complex to operate but maximises BitMEX-bound flow while keeping capital requirements proportional. Not recommended for the initial launch but useful as an evolution path.

Recommendation

DLT's working preference

Suggested direction

DLT proposes Option 1 (Riskless Principal / Best Execution) as the initial operating model, conditional on BitMEX and Ouinex coordinating the back-routing arrangement described in steps 1 and 5 above. This model delivers the simplest fit with EU broker obligations, requires the least capital, and routes effectively 100% of executed volume to BitMEX Global.

Option 2 remains on the roadmap as a future state for higher-margin client tiers and for symbols where DLT wants to retain inventory upside, but launching as a prop trader from day one increases both regulatory and operational complexity without immediately enlarging BitMEX-bound flow.

We would welcome BitMEX's view on (a) the back-routing arrangement with Ouinex, (b) fee economics under each model, and (c) whether BitMEX has internal preferences shaped by precedent from similar EU broker integrations.
