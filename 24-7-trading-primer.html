24/7 Trading of Equities, Metals
and Commodities — CFD & Spot

Global markets are shut roughly 50 hours a week, plus every public holiday. Demand isn't. This primer sets out the size of the weekend gap, why tokenised equities and RWA perpetuals now make round-the-clock pricing viable, and how DLT sources liquidity from crypto venues and pipes it into a bank or broker's existing CFD and spot rails.

TL;DR

Weekend and out-of-hours trading is the single largest piece of unserved demand in retail and wealth distribution. TradFi can't price it — the underlying exchange is closed, so there is no reference price and no hedge.

Two things changed. Tokenised equities (xStocks, Ondo Global Markets) give a continuously-traded spot reference. RWA perpetuals on DLT-native venues give a deep, continuously-funded derivatives book on indices, single names, metals and commodities — with real weekend volume.

That means a bank, broker or CFD provider can now quote a two-way price at 03:00 on a Sunday and actually hedge it. DLT's role is the bridge: aggregate the crypto-side liquidity, run the risk book, and deliver a clean, MiFID-wrapped price and settlement into the client's existing front end.

01

The weekend gap

A European or US broker's tradable week is about 118 hours out of 168. Equity venues are open ~32.5h. Everything else — weekends, overnight, holidays — is dead air on the P&L, but it is not dead air for the client. News doesn't respect the closing bell: earnings leaks, geopolitics, central bank speeches, commodity supply shocks and crypto-led risk-off all happen when the tape is shut.

The client experience today is: something happens Saturday, they can see it, they can't act on it, and they carry gap risk into Monday's open. Every broker in the market has the same hole. Whoever closes it first takes the flow — and, more importantly, takes the account, because 24/7 is a reason to move the whole relationship, not just one trade.

The structural point

24/7 isn't a feature. It's a distribution wedge. The first broker in a market to offer weekend equity and commodity exposure doesn't compete on spread — it competes on availability, which is a category nobody else is in.

Read

Commodities and metals are the weekend engine. Oil, gas and metals react to weekend supply and geopolitical news and are natively 24/7-speculated — they hold nearly half their weekday activity. Indices hold ~1/6. Single-name equities die on weekends (10.3%) precisely because there is no reference price when the primary exchange is shut — which is the gap tokenised equity spot now fills.

And this is one venue, at an early point in the RWA perp cycle. Weekend absolute notional grew from ~$0.7B to ~$1.9B per week across the six-week window while the weekend share stayed flat at 4–7% — i.e. the weekend is growing at the same rate as the whole market, not saturating.

03

The revenue math for banks, brokers and CFD providers

The economics are simple because the cost base is already paid for. Front end, client base, licence, compliance, custody — all exist. Weekend trading is incremental revenue on a fixed cost base, which is why the margin on it is structurally better than weekday flow.

Where the money really is

The headline "+4–6% volume" understates it. In the weekend window you are typically the only venue quoting the name, so realised spread is a multiple of the weekday number. A weekend book doing 12% of weekday volume at 3× the spread earns roughly a third of a weekday's revenue — on a day that currently earns zero.

Specifically for CFD brokers

CFD is the cleanest fit, because a CFD is already a synthetic — it never needed the underlying to settle, only to price and hedge. The two blockers were (a) no reference price at the weekend and (b) no hedge venue. RWA perps solve both simultaneously: they are a continuously-marked, continuously-funded synthetic on the same underlying, and they accept size.

  • Pricing: perp mid + funding-adjusted basis becomes the weekend reference.
  • Hedging: net client exposure is offset directly on the perp venue, in USDC, instantly, at any hour.
  • Financing: perp funding rate is an observable, market-set input to the weekend swap rate — defensible to both client and regulator.
  • Risk: the broker warehouses less, not more, because a hedge venue exists on the days it previously didn't.
04

Why this works now and didn't three years ago

24/7 equity trading has been proposed repeatedly and failed every time, for one reason: you cannot quote what you cannot hedge. A weekend price with no hedge venue is a bet by the broker against its own clients — which no risk committee approves and no regulator likes. Three things changed.

Tokenised equity spot became continuously traded xStocks (Backed) and Ondo Global Markets issue 1:1 collateralised, on-chain representations of US equities and ETFs that trade on DEXs and CEXs around the clock. That produces a continuous, observable spot reference for names that would otherwise be dark from Friday close to Monday open — and a physical leg for genuine spot delivery.
RWA perpetuals reached real depth Permissionless perp DEXs now list indices, single names, precious metals and energy with continuous funding and, per §02, material weekend volume. This is the hedge venue that did not exist. It is deep enough to absorb a broker's net book, not just retail-sized clips.
Stablecoin settlement removed the weekend cash constraint Even with a price and a hedge, weekend trading used to break on settlement: no wires, no margin calls, no funding movement Saturday. USDC settles in seconds, 24/7. Collateral can move, margin can be topped up, and hedges can be funded on a Sunday.
The regulatory wrapper caught up Securitised structures — the subject of our companion perps primer — let the crypto-native exposure arrive at the bank as a listed, ISIN-bearing, MiFID-eligible instrument. The client-facing bank never touches an on-chain venue, a wallet, or a token. That is the piece that makes this deployable inside a regulated institution rather than adjacent to one.

05

How DLT sources liquidity from DeFi and moves it into TradFi

This is the core mechanic. Liquidity exists in crypto-native venues. Demand exists in regulated distribution. Neither can touch the other directly — a bank can't hold a perp position on an offshore DEX, and a DEX can't onboard a Sparkasse client. DLT sits in the middle and performs four transformations: venue, format, currency, and legal wrapper.

Walking one weekend trade end to end

Saturday 14:20 — client acts Retail client of a CFD broker sees weekend headlines on oil and buys €50k of a crude CFD in the broker's normal app. Nothing about the UX is different.
Price comes from the DLT mark The broker quotes off DLT's synthetic reference: perp mid, funding-adjusted, cross-checked against tokenised spot where one exists. Timestamped and auditable for best-execution reporting.
Internalisation first DLT nets that buy against other clients' weekend sells across the whole book. Only the residual delta needs an external hedge — most flow never leaves the engine.
Residual hedged on-chain Net long delta is offset on the RWA perp venue in USDC, in seconds, at 14:21 on a Saturday. This is the step that was structurally impossible before.
Funding and margin clear over the weekend Perp funding accrues continuously and feeds the client's weekend swap rate. Margin moves in stablecoin — no Monday-morning wire dependency.
Monday open — convergence, not a gap When the primary exchange reopens, DLT rolls the hedge from perp into conventional futures/cash if desired. The client already has their position at a fair weekend price rather than gapping into the open.

Integration reality

For most partners this is a price feed plus a hedge counterparty, not a platform migration. The broker keeps its front end, its licence and its client relationship. DLT supplies the 24/7 mark, absorbs the risk it can't warehouse, and settles in a currency and wrapper its back office already understands. That is the shortest possible path to live.

07

Risks and honest limits

This works, but it is not free of hard edges. Anyone evaluating it should price these in rather than discover them in month three.

What genuinely works today

  • Commodities, metals and indices have real weekend depth — 44%, 17% and 16% of weekday per-day volume
  • Continuous, funding-marked reference price exists and is observable
  • Stablecoin settlement removes the weekend cash blocker outright
  • Securitised wrapper is a solved problem — see the perps primer
  • Incremental revenue lands on an already-paid-for cost base

What to be careful about

  • Weekend spreads are genuinely wider. Don't promise weekday pricing; price the window honestly
  • Basis risk. Perp mark can drift from Monday's cash open; the gap is smaller than an unhedged gap but not zero
  • Single-name depth is thin outside mega-caps — launch narrow
  • Venue concentration. Most RWA perp depth sits on very few venues; that is counterparty and operational risk
  • Corporate actions over a weekend on a synthetic equity need explicit contractual handling
  • Regulatory posture varies: the wrapper solves instrument eligibility, not every jurisdiction's marketing rules
On the "10% of weekly liquidity" framing

You'll hear weekend liquidity quoted as "about 10% of the week". Be precise about which denominator: measured on our six-week window it is 5.6% of total weekly notional, but 14.9% of a weekday's per-day volume (and 44.5% in commodities). The per-day comparison is the honest one for a revenue case, because it maps directly onto days your book currently earns zero.

08

What we're proposing

Phase 1 — Commodities and metals, weekend only Highest weekend depth, cleanest hedge, no corporate-action complexity, no single-name reference-price problem. Prove the price feed, the hedge loop and the weekend settlement cycle on the easiest asset class.
Phase 2 — Indices, extended to full 24/7 Move from weekend-only to genuinely continuous, including the overnight window that is currently just as dark as the weekend and larger in aggregate hours.
Phase 3 — Mega-cap single names, spot + CFD Add the tokenised spot leg (xStocks / Ondo) so single names have a physical reference and genuine spot delivery, not only a synthetic mark.
Phase 4 — Securitised certificate distribution Package the whole thing as an ISIN instrument so banks that cannot hold derivatives directly can still distribute the exposure through standard custody.
The one-line case

The liquidity to price equities, metals and commodities around the clock already exists — it's just sitting in venues a regulated broker cannot reach. DLT's job is to reach it, transform it, and hand it over in a format the broker's existing stack already accepts. The broker gets 104 extra trading days a year at a multiple of weekday spread, on infrastructure it has already paid for.
