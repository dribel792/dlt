What everyone is doing about it
  • Crypto-native players are widening product scope fast — not to win spot, but to hold the deposit. Every additional product is a reason for a client not to move their balance.
  • Two axes of expansion, run in parallel: new asset classes (equities, metals, commodities, debt, prediction markets) and new product types (leverage, yield, downside protection).
  • TradFi is coming the other way — banks, brokers and CFD houses now want the 24/7 availability that only crypto rails deliver, wrapped so it arrives as a regulated instrument.
  • Nobody is buying a licence any more; they are buying a shelf. A distributor picks the counterparty that can list the widest range of products fastest, on the widest range of underlyings.
  • The two flows meet in the middle. That intersection is a licensing, risk and clearing problem — which is precisely the part DLT already owns.

And this is one venue

Hyperliquid xyz DEX, 1d candles, 22 Jun → 02 Aug 2026. Weekend = Sat + Sun UTC. Bars scaled to 50% max.
Why they cannot build it themselves
  • You cannot quote what you cannot hedge. A weekend price with no hedge venue is the broker betting against its own clients. No risk committee signs that off.
  • RWA perps are now the hedge venue. Continuously funded, continuously marked, deep enough to absorb a broker's net book — not just retail clips.
  • Tokenised equity spot gives the reference price when the primary exchange is dark — xStocks, Ondo, 1:1 collateralised, on-chain.
  • Stablecoins removed the settlement blocker. Margin can move on a Sunday. No wire, no cut-off.
  • But a bank cannot hold a perp on an offshore DEX. It needs the exposure to arrive as an ISIN-bearing, MiFID-eligible instrument. That transformation is the product we sell.
Our position in this trade

Liquidity sits in crypto venues. Demand sits in regulated distribution. Neither can touch the other directly. DLT sits in the middle and performs four transformations — venue, format, currency and legal wrapper — and charges for all four.

Why the perp is the shape that won
  • No expiry, no roll. A retail user never has to understand contango, a futures curve, or a settlement calendar. That single simplification is why perps beat listed futures for retail.
  • Funding rate does the work. The contract self-anchors to spot through a payment between longs and shorts. No exchange has to manage the basis.
  • One contract shape, every asset class. The same engine that prices a BTC perp prices a gold perp or an NVDA perp. Marginal cost of a new market is close to zero.
  • It is the gateway product. Users arrive for spot, discover leverage, and never go back. Every downstream leverage product we sell — turbos, mini futures, securitised perps — is sold to a user who first traded a perp.
  • The perp is also our hedge venue. The same instrument that we sell to clients is the one we use to lay off risk on securitised perps and 24/7 CFDs. One liquidity relationship, three revenue lines.
Strategic point

Perps are not a product line. They are the top of the leverage funnel. Whoever owns the perp owns the customer for everything above it.

What this means for the strategy
  • Keep investing in spot and Delta-1 — as acquisition. It is 13% of revenue and 100% of the funnel. It does not need to pay for itself directly.
  • Leverage is the P&L. Fund it first. Securitised perps with Nexo in Q4 is the single highest-value item on the roadmap.
  • Yield products hold the balance between leverage trades. They are cheap to add on the shared backbone and each one raises blended revenue per user.
  • Every new asset class multiplies the shelf. One risk engine × six asset classes × three product categories. That grid is the business.
The ask

Endorse the sequencing: securitised perps → 24/7 metals & commodities → structured shelf on new underlyings. Everything else is downstream of the MiFID risk engine we already have.
