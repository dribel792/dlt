03 Framing · Where it fits

It is a Turbo/KO certificate — with three novel edges

Most of this is 20+ years of established German Turbo/KO practice. The scrutiny — and this workshop — is about the parts that are new.

Same as a classic Turbo / KO cert

  • Leveraged instrument with a knock-out barrier
  • Transferable security — bearer, ISIN, cash-settled
  • Not a CFD — established German precedent since ~2003
  • PRIIPs Category 3, per-Series KID
  • Barrier fixed in Final Terms, distributed via MiFID firms
  • Multi-asset issuance (crypto, metals, equities) already market practice

What is new / draws scrutiny

  • Open-ended / perpetual as a systematic construct for TradFi underlyings
  • Venue mark-price reference (Bybit/BitMEX) → BMR question
  • Dual issuance — DLT security token and bearer cert; fungibility TBC
  • Funding/carry passthrough via dynamic barrier
  • Add-margin top-up and non-USD collateral
  • Free wallet-to-wallet transfer after issuance-gate KYC

Takeaway for counsel: we are not inventing a new legal category — we are extending a proven one. The workshop pressure-tests only the right-hand column.

04 Classification · The Thesis

One sentence the whole product rests on

"A Perpetual KO Certificate is a transferable security, not a CFD."

Legal basis

Transferable security under MiFID II Art. 4(1)(44). PRIIPs Category 3. Explicitly outside ESMA CFD intervention 2018/796.

Rationale

20+ year German Turbo/KO precedent as regulated securities — issued as bearer cert or security token, cash-settled, no contingent liability.

Why it is existential

If reclassified as a bilateral OTC derivative → 2× retail crypto leverage cap → the product, as designed, is dead.

Ask of counsel (15 min in room): endorse or challenge this thesis out loud. Nominate the owner for a written classification opinion. If they don't fully buy it, nothing else on the agenda matters.

05 Classification · The 3 Kill-Shots

Three risks that can actually end the product

Not all 17 open questions are equal. These three can force reclassification or block distribution. Spend the most time here.

KILL-SHOT 1

CFD reclassification

The add-margin top-up (Q6) and bidirectional / negative-funding barrier (Q12) are the mechanics most arguable as "individual negotiation" or a "bilateral obligation."

→ if either forces CFD status, retail caps at 2×.

KILL-SHOT 2

BMR / benchmark

Using exchange mark prices (Bybit, BitMEX) as the reference. Is that a "benchmark" under BMR Art. 3(1)(3)? Does the regulated-exchange exemption hold?

→ need a clear answer or a fallback (BMR index / spot).

KILL-SHOT 3

Free transfer vs AML

KYC at issuance only, then wallet-to-wallet with no per-transfer KYC. Does this survive EU AML/CFT? Is issuer sanctions-freeze + holder register enough?

→ this is where a regulator says no.

Each kill-shot leaves with a named owner and a date. Everything else is refinement; these three are go / no-go.
