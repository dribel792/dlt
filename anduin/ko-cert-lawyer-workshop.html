03 Mechanic 1 of 8 · Non-USD Collateral

Margin can be posted in crypto or tokenised gold — not just USD

How we build it

The holder posts margin in-kind — USDC, BTC, ETH or tokenised gold (XAUt). Collateral is valued continuously against the same reference feed used for the certificate, with a volatility haircut. Settlement currency stays USD/EUR per Final Terms, but the economic margin can be crypto-denominated. Posted collateral sits with the Kryptoverwahr-licensed custodian from day one; the issuer deposits matching margin into its hedge to stay flat.

Why it matters

This mirrors cross / isolated margin on a native exchange — the trader funds a position with the asset they already hold, without a USD conversion. It is core to the "feels exactly like a perp" objective.

Legal · does accepting variable crypto collateral affect the transferable-security character or PRIIPs treatment? Ties to S1 (token custody) and Q9.

04 Mechanic 2 of 8 · Add Margin (Top-Up)

Depositing collateral moves the barrier away and lowers leverage

How we build it

The holder deposits additional collateral. The barrier is moved further from the mark, which lowers implied leverage and pushes knock-out away. The issuer simultaneously tops up its hedge to preserve back-to-back alignment. Economically identical to adding margin to a perp position.

New Barrier = Prev Barrier − (ΔC / Size / #Certs) // long
Leverage = Mark ÷ (Mark − Barrier) // falls as barrier drops
MAIN RISK · Q6

The legal question

Does an individual margin top-up constitute "individual negotiation of terms," and does that create CFD reclassification risk? This is one of the three go / no-go questions.

Alternative if needed: mint into a new series at the new barrier rather than adjust the existing one in place.

05 Mechanic 3 of 8 · Maintenance-Margin Buffer

A deterministic cash floor for exact perp-parity liquidation

How we build it

An optional per-series parameter. The barrier is placed at the zero-equity level, and a fixed cash buffer (0.3–1.0% of notional) is paid to the holder at knock-out — so a "liquidation" leaves the exact residual a perp would, not a hard zero.

Barrier = Mark × (1 − 1/Leverage)
Residual@KO = max(0,(Mark−Barrier)×Size) + MMB

Worked example

10× BTC, MMB 0.5%, notional ≈ $102.40 per cert:

· Standard clean hit → $0.00
· With MMB → $0.51 deterministic floor

Legal · Q15 — PRIIPs cost-vs-payoff treatment; specifiable per-series in Final Terms without amending the base prospectus.

06 Mechanic 4 of 8 · Funding / Carry

A leveraged security that can pay its holder

How we build it

Carry is passed through by moving the barrier every settlement — 8-hourly for crypto, daily for TradFi. It is bidirectional: positive funding drifts the barrier against the holder (a cost); negative funding drifts it in the holder's favour — the holder is effectively paid, exactly like receiving funding on a perp.

Crypto: New Barrier = Prev ± (Funding × Mark)
TradFi: ± ESTR/SOFR ± dividend / roll yield

Why this is notable

Most structured products only ever charge the holder. Here, a negative-funding series credits the holder an ongoing carry — we are building an instrument that can pay its owner, not just cost them.

Legal · Q1 & Q12 (MAIN RISK). Q1 — how precisely must the barrier-adjustment formula be disclosed, and what update flexibility remains? Q12 — does crediting the holder (barrier decrease = effective credit) look like a bilateral financial obligation → CFD? Plus PRIIPs disclosure of negative costs.

07 Mechanic 5 of 8 · Leverage 1–30×

Leverage expressed purely through barrier placement

How we build it

Leverage is chosen at issuance and expressed entirely by where the barrier sits — no margin account, no contingent liability. Implied leverage then drifts continuously as the price moves (never constant — identical to a perp). The user picks a tier; we issue the series with the matching barrier.

Leverage = Mark ÷ (Mark − Barrier)

The tier question

High tiers (20–30×) are the sensitive band. We expect to restrict them to professional clients.

Legal · Q7 — product-intervention risk for 20–30× crypto in DE / AT / NL, and PRIIPs adverse-scenario adequacy for extreme leverage.

08 Mechanic 6 of 8 · Knock-Out Barrier

The barrier is the liquidation — and the anchor of the thesis

How we build it

The classic Turbo / KO mechanic: automatic termination the moment the mark reaches the barrier. Long — barrier below, breached as price falls to it. Short — barrier above, breached as price rises to it. Cash settlement of the residual.

Residual = max(0,(Mark@KO − Barrier) × Size) // long

Typically ≈ 0 on a clean hit, or the MMB floor where enabled.

Why it is the anchor

This exact mechanic is 20+ years of established German Turbo / KO precedent as a regulated security, not a CFD. It is the strongest single argument for our whole classification.

Legal · Q5 — gap risk: the issuer's obligation and residual calculation when the mark price gaps through the barrier.

09 Mechanic 7 of 8 · Perpetual / Open-Ended

No expiry — the carry passthrough is what makes it sustainable

How we build it

There is no fixed maturity — the certificate lives until knock-out or the holder exits in the secondary market. The carry passthrough (Mechanic 4) is precisely what makes an open-ended leveraged security viable: the issuer recovers financing continuously via the barrier, so no roll or expiry is needed. A 24/7 reference and 24/7 hedge (crypto perps) remove overnight gaps.

Novelty profile

Open-ended leverage is established for crypto mini-futures since 2018. What is new is running it as a systematic programme, including for TradFi underlyings.

Legal · Q2 (fungible series & tap issuance) and Q8 (unlimited-series base prospectus) — documenting open-ended, tappable series under one base.

10 Mechanic 8 of 8 · Multi-Asset

One base prospectus across four asset classes

How we build it

A single base prospectus spans crypto, precious metals, equities and commodities. Each series' Final Terms pick the underlying and reference venue. Preferred reference is a 24/7 perp mark price where one exists; fallback is spot / futures. Hedging runs via crypto perps for a 24/7 net-flat book, with prime-broker / futures / LBMA fallbacks.

Reference & hedge

· Crypto → venue perp mark (BitMEX, Bybit…)
· Metals / equities / commodities → perp where liquid, else spot / futures / index

Legal · Q17 (multi-asset perimeter), Q16 (per-series venue election), Q20 (index licensing & equity reference data) — plus a separate BMR analysis per reference source.

11 Where It Fits

It is a Turbo / KO certificate — with three novel edges

Most of this is 20+ years of established German Turbo / KO practice. The scrutiny — and this workshop — is about the parts that are new.

Same as a classic Turbo / KO cert

  • Leveraged instrument with a knock-out barrier
  • Transferable security — bearer, ISIN, cash-settled
  • Not a CFD — established German precedent since ~2003
  • PRIIPs Category 3, per-Series KID
  • Barrier fixed in Final Terms, distributed via MiFID firms
  • Multi-asset issuance already market practice

What is new / draws scrutiny

  • Open-ended / perpetual as a systematic construct for TradFi underlyings
  • Venue mark-price reference (Bybit / BitMEX) → BMR question
  • Dual issuance — DLT security token and bearer cert; fungibility TBC
  • Funding / carry passthrough via dynamic barrier that can pay the holder
  • Add-margin top-up and non-USD collateral
  • Free wallet-to-wallet transfer after issuance-gate KYC

Takeaway for counsel: we are not inventing a new legal category — we are extending a proven one. The workshop pressure-tests only the right-hand column.

12 The Main Risks

Three questions that can actually end the product

The full register (next three slides) has S1–S9 and Q1–Q30. These three are the go / no-go items — everything rests on the sentence: "a KO Certificate is a transferable security, not a CFD."

MAIN RISK 1 · Q6 / Q12

CFD reclassification

The add-margin top-up (Q6) and bidirectional / negative-funding barrier (Q12) are the mechanics most arguable as "individual negotiation" or a "bilateral obligation."

→ if either forces CFD status, retail crypto caps at 2×.

MAIN RISK 2 · Q11

BMR / benchmark

Using exchange mark prices (Bybit, BitMEX) as the reference. Is that a "benchmark" under BMR? Does the regulated-exchange exemption hold?

→ need a clear answer or a fallback index / spot.

MAIN RISK 3 · S4

Free transfer vs AML

KYC at issuance only, then wallet-to-wallet with no per-transfer KYC. Does this survive EU AML/CFT? Is issuer freeze + register enough?

→ this is where a regulator says no.

Each main risk leaves the room with a named owner and a date. The remaining register items matter, but these three are existential.
