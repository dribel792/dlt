PRIIPs KID & Final Terms Generator
Technical Build Outline

What we have to build to issue perpetual knock-out certificates at scale: document generation, the risk & cost calculation engine behind it, and the filing connection into the FMA.
Date23 October 2026 AudienceEngineering & Product — for effort/duration assessment Programme contextPerpetual KO Certificate Programme (see Programme Term Sheet) StatusDraft for sizing — not a spec
Why this exists. Every certificate Series we issue needs two legal documents at the moment of issuance: the Final Terms (Prospectus Regulation) and the PRIIPs KID (Key Information Document). Competitors issue thousands of Series per day, fully automated, in minutes. Manual document production caps us at a handful of products. This generator is the issuance factory — without it there is no scalable product shelf. The purpose of this doc is to let eng/product size the lift and put a timeline on it.

2System overview

1 — Product Master (golden source)Series parameters entered/created via API or issuance UI → validated against base prospectus constraints → ISIN requested → Series record becomes the single source of truth for every downstream artifact.
2 — Market Data LayerPrice history, perp funding rates, FX and rates curves ingested from reference venues + market data vendor; quality-checked and stored as the input set for all calculations.
3 — Risk & Cost Calculation EngineComputes SRI (market + credit risk), four performance scenarios at the required holding periods, and the full PRIIPs cost tables — per Series, reproducibly, with audit trail.
4 — Document GenerationTemplating engine merges Series data + calc results into the Final Terms form (annexed to the base prospectus) and the fixed 3-page KID layout; renders PDF per language; four-eyes approval where required.
5 — Filing & PublicationFinal Terms filed with the FMA; KID published on issuer website; both pushed to distribution partners and reference-data vendors; passporting notifications where we sell cross-border.
6 — Ongoing MonitoringDaily/monthly jobs re-check every live Series: has the SRI moved? Scenarios stale? Costs changed? KO event? → regenerate and republish the KID, or terminate the Series record.

3The calculation engine (the real quant lift)

3.1 Summary Risk Indicator (SRI)

SRI = the 1–7 risk number on page 1 of the KID. It combines a Market Risk Measure (MRM) and a Credit Risk Measure (CRM):

  • MRM (Category 3): bootstrap-simulate the product's value distribution over the recommended holding period from at least 2 years of daily observed returns (5 years preferred per RTS) — minimum 10,000 paths. Compute VaR at 97.5%, convert to VaR-equivalent volatility (VEV), map VEV to MRM class 1–7. For 10–30× KO certs this will land at 7 almost always, but we must compute it, not assert it.
  • KO handling: simulated paths must respect the knock-out — a path that touches the barrier terminates at residual value (incl. MMB). This is bespoke logic; off-the-shelf PRIIPs calculators generally don't model our zero-equity barrier + MMB payout.
  • CRM: derived from the issuer SPV's credit standing (unrated → CRM per RTS fallback rules; collateralisation structure may be relevant). Mostly a static mapping, low lift.

3.2 Performance scenarios

Four scenarios (stress / unfavourable / moderate / favourable) at the recommended holding period, from the same simulated distribution (prescribed percentiles). Open-end product → RHP definition needs a product/legal decision (likely 1 year intermediate-style treatment). Scenario values must show what a retail investor gets back on €10,000, net of costs, with KO paths flowing through to the stress/unfavourable numbers.

3.3 Cost calculations

Full PRIIPs cost tables: entry/exit (bid-offer, issue premium), ongoing (financing spread on the financing level, management fee if any), expressed both as total costs and as annual cost impact on return. Two product-specific wrinkles flagged in the lawyer workshop that the engine must support:

  • Negative carry/costs: when the funding rate flows to the holder, the "cost" can be negative — methodology for disclosing this is a pending counsel question; the engine should be parameterised either way.
  • Variable funding rate: the barrier adjustment depends on the reference venue's funding rate → ongoing cost figures are estimates that must be refreshed, not constants.
SRI = max( MRM(VEV from 10k bootstrapped KO-aware paths), CRM(issuer) )
scenario(p) = percentile_p( net payoff distribution over RHP, after costs )

3.4 Ongoing review (this is a system, not a one-shot)

The RTS requires KID review whenever a change would materially affect the information, and at minimum on a defined schedule: in practice a monthly MRM recheck across every live Series plus event triggers (reference venue change, funding regime change, cost change, corporate-action equivalents). With a shelf of thousands of Series this is a scheduled pipeline with diffing, regeneration and republication — budget it as a standing subsystem, not a cron afterthought.

4Final Terms generator

  • Template is fixed by the base prospectus: the "form of Final Terms" is an annex of the approved base prospectus — the generator fills blanks, it may not add or alter disclosure. Tight coupling to the legal drafting: template changes require prospectus supplement awareness.
  • Inputs: entirely from the Product Master (Section 2, step 1) — no free text at generation time. Per-Series venue election, calibration choice, MMB amount, carry mechanism per asset class all flow from validated fields.
  • Validation layer: parameter bounds from the prospectus (max leverage, eligible underlyings, eligible reference venues, min issue size) enforced at Series creation so an invalid Series cannot reach document generation.
  • Output: PDF for filing/publication + structured version (XML/JSON) for distributors and vendors. Immutable versioning + audit trail (who created, who approved, what was filed when).

5Connection into the FMA

Setting expectations There is no real-time "FMA API" in the sense of a trading integration. The connection is a filing workflow: Final Terms must be filed with the approving NCA (FMA Liechtenstein — or CSSF Luxembourg; the election is still open per the Programme Term Sheet, so build this as a pluggable adapter) each time a Series is issued. The KID itself is not approved by the regulator — our obligation is publication and maintenance.
  • Final Terms filing: submission via the NCA's e-service/portal channel (FMA e-Service; CSSF uses its own filing portal). Expect semi-automated at best: a filing adapter that packages the PDF + metadata and either submits via the portal's supported upload mechanism or queues for a one-click human submission. Clarify with the NCA during the pre-submission meetings what machine-friendly channels exist — this determines whether this component is days or weeks.
  • Base prospectus + supplements: separate, lawyer-driven approval track — out of scope for the generator, but the generator must know which prospectus version each Series completes.
  • Passporting: NCA notifies host-state regulators for cross-border offers; our side is tracking which Series/documents are passported where, and producing host-language KIDs where required.
  • Publication duties: Final Terms and KID published on the issuer website (versioned, retrievable historical versions); KID available before any retail transaction. Distributors will also demand a document feed (API/SFTP) — brokers won't screen-scrape our website.

7Build vs. buy

PRIIPs calc + KID production vendors exist and most mid-size issuers use them. Honest assessment of the trade:

  • Buy/hybrid: fastest route to compliant KIDs; but our KO-barrier-with-MMB payoff, perp funding reference and possible negative costs are non-standard — vendor onboarding will involve custom payoff work either way, and per-document pricing hurts at thousands of Series.
  • Build: the calc engine (rows 3–4) is the only genuinely hard part, and it overlaps heavily with pricing/hedging analytics we need in-house anyway. Everything else is standard data + document plumbing.
  • Recommendation to assess: build the engine and generators, consider buying only the regulatory-template maintenance (RTS wording updates per language) as a content subscription.

8Open questions blocking final sizing

  1. NCA election (FMA vs. CSSF) and what their portals actually support for automated Final Terms filing — ask in the pre-submission meetings.
  2. RHP treatment for an open-end product — product/legal decision, drives scenario methodology.
  3. Counsel answers on negative-cost disclosure and scenario validity under variable funding (lawyer workshop Qs) — parameterise the engine, don't wait, but these gate go-live.
  4. Launch languages beyond EN/DE (depends on passporting targets).
  5. Expected issuance volume at launch (10s vs. 1000s of Series/day) — changes infra sizing for rows 1, 8, 9, not the architecture.
