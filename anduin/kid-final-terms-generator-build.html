PRIIPs classification — Category 1 (working basis) This doc is written on the basis that the certificates are PRIIPs Category 1 securitised derivatives (Annex II, point 4(b) of the RTS — securitised derivatives such as warrants and knock-out products are treated as falling within MiFID II Annex I Section C items 4–10; this is the standard classification used by KO/turbo issuers across the industry). Category 1 means the Market Risk Measure is fixed at 7 — no Monte Carlo / bootstrap simulation infrastructure is required for the SRI. The W&C base prospectus draft is silent on PRIIPs, so this classification must be confirmed in writing by counsel before build completion; it materially changes the quant scope (Section 3).

3The calculation engine

3.1 Summary Risk Indicator (SRI)

SRI = the 1–7 risk number on page 1 of the KID, combining a Market Risk Measure (MRM) and a Credit Risk Measure (CRM):

  • MRM: under Category 1, point 4(b), the MRM is assigned as 7 by rule — no VaR simulation, no return-history requirement. This removes the heaviest quant component entirely; the engine just records the classification basis per Series for the audit trail.
  • CRM: derived from the issuer SPV's credit standing (unrated → RTS fallback rules; the collateral/custody structure may be relevant to mitigation). A static mapping maintained per issuer, low lift.
  • Net effect: SRI = 7 for every Series; the engine's job is producing it defensibly (documented basis, versioned), not computing it.

3.2 Performance scenarios

Four scenarios (stress / unfavourable / moderate / favourable) at the recommended holding period. For Category 1 securitised derivatives these are payoff-formula based: apply prescribed underlying moves to the certificate's payoff function — leverage via Multiplier/Strike, funding settlement accrual, and the knock-out: any scenario path that touches the barrier terminates at residual value. The KO logic is bespoke to our product (zero-equity barrier calibration, funding-dependent barrier adjustment) but it is deterministic payoff evaluation, not simulation. RHP for an open-end product needs a product decision (KO certs are typically presented with short/intraday RHPs).

3.3 Cost calculations

Full PRIIPs cost tables: entry/exit (bid-offer, issue premium), ongoing (financing spread on the strike/financing level, management fee if any), expressed both as total costs and as annual cost impact on return. Two product-specific wrinkles the engine must support:

  • Negative carry/costs: when the funding settlement flows to the holder, the "cost" can be negative — the engine should be parameterised to disclose either direction.
  • Variable funding rate: ongoing cost figures depend on the Reference Body's funding rate → they are estimates that must be refreshed, not constants.

3.4 Ongoing review (this is a system, not a one-shot)

The RTS requires KID review whenever a change would materially affect the information, and on a defined minimum schedule. Under Category 1 the SRI never moves, which makes the recheck cheap — but scenario figures and cost estimates still go stale (funding regime changes, spread changes, venue changes), and KO events must close out the Series record and withdraw its documents. With a shelf of thousands of Series this is a scheduled pipeline with diffing, regeneration and republication — budget it as a standing subsystem, not a cron afterthought.

4Final Terms generator

  • Template is fixed by the base prospectus: the "form of Final Terms" is an annex of the approved base prospectus — the generator fills blanks, it may not add or alter disclosure. Tight coupling to the legal drafting: template changes require prospectus supplement awareness.
  • Inputs: entirely from the Product Master (Section 2, step 1) — no free text at generation time. Underlying + identifiers, direction, multiplier, initial strike, funding settlement cadence and Reference Body all flow from validated fields.
  • Validation layer: parameter bounds from the prospectus (max leverage, eligible underlyings, eligible Reference Bodies, issuance volume) enforced at Series creation so an invalid Series cannot reach document generation.
  • Output: PDF for filing/publication + structured version (XML/JSON) for distributors. Immutable versioning + audit trail (who created, who approved, what was filed when).

5Connection into the FMA

Setting expectations There is no real-time "FMA API" in the sense of a trading integration. The connection is a filing workflow: Final Terms must be filed with the approving NCA each time a Series is issued. Build it as a pluggable adapter in case the NCA election changes.
  • Final Terms filing: submission via the NCA's e-service/portal channel. Expect semi-automated at best: a filing adapter that packages the PDF + metadata and either submits via the portal's supported upload mechanism or queues for a one-click human submission. Clarify with the FMA during the pre-submission meetings what machine-friendly channels exist — this determines whether this component is days or weeks.
  • Base prospectus + supplements: separate, lawyer-driven approval track — out of scope for the generator, but the generator must know which prospectus version each Series completes.
  • Passporting: the NCA notifies host-state regulators for cross-border offers; our side is tracking which Series/documents are passported where, and producing host-language KIDs where required.
  • Publication duties: Final Terms and KID published on the issuer website (versioned, retrievable historical versions); KID available before any retail transaction. Distributors will also demand a document feed (API/SFTP) — brokers won't screen-scrape our website.
