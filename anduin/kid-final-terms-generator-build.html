The Securities are issued as blockchain-native security tokens sui generis and are not listed on a traditional venue — the base prospectus contains no ISIN; the per-Series identifier is the "Securities identifier" set in the Final Terms. No numbering-agency integration, no exchange reference-data feeds.

PRIIPs classification — Category 3 (confirmed by W&C, 27 Oct 2026) Counsel has confirmed the certificates are PRIIPs Category 3 — not Category 1 as earlier working drafts assumed. Consequences, per W&C: (1) the MRM must be computed via the full Annex II bootstrapped historical simulation — at least 10,000 simulated paths, VaR at 97.5% confidence, built on at least 2 years of daily prices of the underlying Perp Futures Contract — with performance scenarios likewise simulation-derived. No deterministic shortcut exists: the only simulation exemption applies to products with unconditional capital protection, which KO certificates do not have. (2) Where a newly listed underlying lacks the required price history, a sufficiently representative spot price / venue index can serve as a documented benchmark/proxy to hold Category 3 while history accrues — but representativeness must be assessed and documented per underlying, case by case (standing item on the listing checklist, not a one-time memo). (3) W&C support only the descriptive parts of the KID and take no responsibility for the issuer's calculations — the quant engine and its validation are entirely ours; a one-time methodology review by a KID provider is the planned mitigation.

2System overview

2 — Market Data LayerUnderlying futures prices, perp funding rates and FX ingested from the Reference Bodies/venues; quality-checked and stored as the input set for scenario and cost calculations. Under Category 3 this layer carries real weight: a clean 2-year daily price history per underlying feeds the bootstrap, plus proxy-fallback logic (spot price / venue index) with a documented representativeness assessment for newly listed underlyings.
3 — Risk & Cost Calculation EngineComputes the SRI (MRM via the Category 3 bootstrapped historical simulation + credit risk measure), simulation-derived performance scenarios with KO-aware path evaluation, and the full PRIIPs cost tables — per Series, reproducibly, with audit trail.
4 — Document GenerationTemplating engine merges Series data + calc results into the Final Terms form (annexed to the base prospectus) and the fixed 3-page KID layout; renders PDF per language; four-eyes approval where required.
5 — Filing & PublicationFinal Terms filed with the FMA; KID and Final Terms published on the issuer website; both pushed to distribution partners.
6 — Ongoing MonitoringScheduled jobs re-check every live Series (scenario staleness, cost changes, KO events → lifecycle handling) and watch for PRIIPs/Prospectus Regulation rule changes that require template or methodology updates.

3The calculation engine

3.1 Summary Risk Indicator (SRI)

SRI = the 1–7 risk number on page 1 of the KID, combining a Market Risk Measure (MRM) and a Credit Risk Measure (CRM):

  • MRM: under Category 3, the MRM is computed via the Annex II bootstrapped historical simulation: resample ≥2 years of daily observed returns of the underlying Perp Futures Contract into ≥10,000 simulated paths, revalue the certificate along each path (leverage via Multiplier/Strike, funding accrual, KO barrier termination), take VaR at 97.5% over the recommended holding period, map the VaR-equivalent volatility to the MRM class. The simulation itself is tractable (vectorised resampling, seconds per Series) — the engineering weight is in the data pipeline and in correct product revaluation inside the sim.
  • CRM: derived from the issuer SPV's credit standing (unrated → RTS fallback rules; the collateral/custody structure may be relevant to mitigation). A static mapping maintained per issuer, low lift.
  • Net effect: given the leverage, the SRI will land at 7 (occasionally 6) in practice — but it is now a computed, movable output, not a rule-assigned constant. The engine must produce it reproducibly (seeded simulation, versioned inputs, audit trail) and the monitoring pipeline must detect when a recomputation shifts the class (Section 3.4).

Proxy handling for young underlyings: where the Perp Futures Contract lacks 2 years of daily history, the engine substitutes a documented benchmark/proxy (spot price or the venue's spot index) per W&C's guidance. Each proxy requires a per-underlying representativeness assessment, stored with the Series record; the proxy is retired once native history accrues. Validation plan: one-time methodology review by a KID provider + sanity-check of outputs against published turbo/KO KIDs from established issuers (same product class — the numbers should rhyme).

3.2 Performance scenarios

Four scenarios (stress / unfavourable / moderate / favourable) at the recommended holding period. Under Category 3 these are simulation-derived, taken from the same bootstrapped path set as the MRM at the percentiles prescribed by the RTS — not deterministic payoff tables. The payoff is linear, but the KO barrier makes revaluation path-dependent: any simulated path that touches the barrier terminates at residual value, so the engine must evaluate barriers along each path, not just at horizon. The KO logic is bespoke to our product (zero-equity barrier calibration, funding-dependent barrier adjustment) — it needs care, not genius. RHP for an open-end product needs a product decision (KO certs are typically presented with short/intraday RHPs; the RHP choice also sets the simulation horizon).

3.3 Cost calculations

Full PRIIPs cost tables: entry/exit (bid-offer, issue premium), ongoing (financing spread on the strike/financing level, management fee if any), expressed both as total costs and as annual cost impact on return. Two product-specific wrinkles the engine must support:

  • Negative carry/costs: when the funding settlement flows to the holder, the "cost" can be negative — the engine should be parameterised to disclose either direction.
  • Variable funding rate: ongoing cost figures depend on the Reference Body's funding rate → they are estimates that must be refreshed, not constants.
MRM = class( VaR₉₇.₅ ( bootstrap ≥10,000 paths · ≥2y daily returns, KO-aware revaluation ) )
SRI = max( MRM, CRM(issuer) )
scenario(p) = percentile_p( simulated KO-aware payoffs over RHP ) − costs

3.4 Ongoing review (this is a system, not a one-shot)

The RTS requires KID review whenever a change would materially affect the information, and on a defined minimum schedule. Under Category 3 the recheck has teeth: the SRI is a computed output, so a shift in the simulated MRM class forces KID republication — the monitoring pipeline must periodically re-run the bootstrap per live Series (rolling the price window forward), diff the SRI and scenario figures against the published KID, and regenerate where material. On top of that, scenario figures and cost estimates go stale (funding regime changes, spread changes, venue changes), proxies must be retired as native history accrues, and KO events must close out the Series record and withdraw its documents. With a shelf of thousands of Series this is a scheduled compute pipeline with diffing, regeneration and republication — budget it as a standing subsystem, not a cron afterthought.

4Final Terms generator

  • Template is fixed by the base prospectus: the "form of Final Terms" is an annex of the approved base prospectus — the generator fills blanks, it may not add or alter disclosure. Tight coupling to the legal drafting: template changes require prospectus supplement awareness.
  • Inputs: entirely from the Product Master (Section 2, step 1) — no free text at generation time. Underlying + identifiers, direction, multiplier, initial strike, funding settlement cadence and Reference Body all flow from validated fields.
  • Validation layer: parameter bounds from the prospectus (max leverage, eligible underlyings, eligible Reference Bodies, issuance volume) enforced at Series creation so an invalid Series cannot reach document generation.
  • Output: PDF for filing/publication + structured version (XML/JSON) for distributors. Immutable versioning + audit trail (who created, who approved, what was filed when).

5Connection into the FMA

Setting expectations There is no real-time "FMA API" in the sense of a trading integration. The connection is a filing workflow: Final Terms must be filed with the approving NCA each time a Series is issued. Build it as a pluggable adapter in case the NCA election changes.
  • Final Terms filing: submission via the NCA's e-service/portal channel. Expect semi-automated at best: a filing adapter that packages the PDF + metadata and either submits via the portal's supported upload mechanism or queues for a one-click human submission. Clarify with the FMA during the pre-submission meetings what machine-friendly channels exist — this determines whether this component is days or weeks.
  • Base prospectus + supplements: separate, lawyer-driven approval track — out of scope for the generator, but the generator must know which prospectus version each Series completes.
  • Passporting: the NCA notifies host-state regulators for cross-border offers; our side is tracking which Series/documents are passported where, and producing host-language KIDs where required.
  • Publication duties: Final Terms and KID published on the issuer website (versioned, retrievable historical versions); KID available before any retail transaction. Distributors will also demand a document feed (API/SFTP) — brokers won't screen-scrape our website.
