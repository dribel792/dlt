Final settlement of dated Series at T* against a 30-minute TWAP of the venue probability. Both formats share the same back-to-back hedge model (issuer hedges directly on the Reference Venue's matching outcome contract), the same security-token-sui generis primary issuance format, the same qualified-custodian integration (Tangany / HAL / BvdH), and the same SPV election (Jersey or Cayman, with Liechtenstein as fallback). Underlyings: outcome probabilities of real-world events published by Kalshi (CFTC-regulated US event-contract exchange) or Polymarket (on-chain prediction market). Why a PM-only Programme — Programme-Architecture Rationale The multi-asset KO Certificate programme (ko-cert-term-sheet.html, v5.2) covers five asset classes (crypto, precious metals, equities, commodities, prediction markets) under a single base prospectus. Bundling PMs into that programme is workable but suboptimal for three reasons. First, the PM asset class is the only one where the underlying is not a financial-instrument price — it is a non-financial-instrument event probability. This triggers a discrete MiFID II Annex I Section C analysis (see Q21-PM / S9-PM) and an ESMA binary-options analysis (Decision EU 2018/795; see Q28-PM) that do not arise for any other asset class. Resolving these within a multi-asset filing risks delaying the entire programme's approval. Second, the dated PM Series uses a structural mechanic — pre-disclosed, NAV-preserving Calendar Restrike Events on the same security token — that has no analogue in the other asset classes. The tax characterisation (Q25-PM), PRIIPS KID modelling across multiple leverage tiers (Q26-PM), MiFID complexity classification (Q27-PM), and BMR posture of the TWAP reference at restrike (Q29-PM) are PM-specific. Third, the gambling-law overlay (Q30-PM) is PM-only; treating it as a footnote within a multi-asset filing risks unprincipled outcomes per jurisdiction. The PM-only programme is structurally identical to the multi-asset programme on every dimension except (a) the asset class (PM-only), (b) the reference and hedge venues (Kalshi / Polymarket only), (c) the formats (Perpetual PM and Dated PM), and (d) the legal-question set (PM-tailored, Q21 + Q25-Q30 promoted to primary; non-PM-specific questions retained where the analysis is identical). The SPV, custody chain, settlement currency, security-token format, transferability principle, MMB calibration, add-margin mechanism, and trading-interface design are all imported unchanged from the multi-asset programme. Where this document refers to a section number or formula identifier that exists in the multi-asset programme primer (e.g., §3.1quinquies, F-Carry, F-AddMargin), the cross-reference is to ko-cert-primer.html and the substantive analysis there applies without modification to the PM-only programme.
Relationship to the multi-asset KO Certificate programme (v5.2) The multi-asset programme treats PMs as one asset class among five and includes the PM-specific Calendar Deleveraging mechanic as a v5.2 addendum. Counsel may, depending on regulatory feedback, elect to either (i) consolidate the PM-only programme into the multi-asset programme as initially intended in v5.0–v5.2, or (ii) keep the PM-only programme as a separate filing alongside the multi-asset programme. This document is drafted on assumption (ii) — that the PM-only programme is filed as a standalone base prospectus. If counsel recommends consolidation, the strategic-context and legal-question sections of this document supersede the corresponding PM coverage in the multi-asset programme (Section S9, Q21, Q25–Q30 of ko-cert-term-sheet.html); the multi-asset programme's coverage of the other asset classes is unaffected.
Note to counsel: This document uses cents (0¢–100¢) to denote prediction-market probability quotes throughout, consistent with Kalshi and Polymarket display conventions. A YES contract quoted at "47¢" is shorthand for an event probability of 47% with a $1 terminal-value reference (i.e., the YES contract pays $1 if the event resolves YES and $0 if it resolves NO; the live market price is the venue's continuously-quoted probability times $1). Worked examples assume the same convention. The perpetual-future analogue from the multi-asset programme — venue mark price as Reference Price, back-to-back hedge on the same venue, deterministic carry passthrough at scheduled Carry Settlement, KO termination on Reference Price reaching Barrier — applies in direct analogy to PMs (probability quote replaces mark price; venue platform fee replaces funding rate; carry is typically zero for PM Series). The Dated PM additionally has a fixed Final Resolution Time T* and a Calendar Deleveraging Schedule (see Term Sheet rows below and §F4 of the formula schedule). A full description of the intended trading interface is provided in Annex A of ko-cert-primer.html; the interface design is unchanged for PM Series, with the asset-class-specific display elements (probability cents, T* countdown for dated Series, calendar restrike indicator) added inline.

Calendar Deleveraging Schedule (Dated PM Series only — programme-principal mechanic)Programme-principal mechanic for dated PM Series. Per-Series election in the Final Terms. The schedule specifies pre-disclosed timestamps before T* at which a NAV-preserving Calendar Restrike Event occurs — the Knock-Out Barrier moves further from the Reference Price, the Contract Size shrinks proportionally so that the cert NAV is preserved at the moment of restrike, and the implied forward leverage cap drops to the next tier. Default schedule (overridable in the Final Terms): T*−30d → cap 3.0×, T*−7d → cap 2.0×, T*−24h → cap 1.0× (effectively spot-equivalent exposure into terminal). The holder's token balance is unchanged across all restrike events; same ISIN, same contract address, same sui-generis security throughout the life of the Series — the restrike is an in-place parameter update, not a token swap or burn-and-reissue. Restrike mechanic is mathematically deterministic (see §F-Restrike below and the supplement pm-cert-calendar-deleveraging-supplement.md for the full worked TRUMP2028 example, smart-contract implementation outline, hedge-book unwind handling, and the open tax / PRIIPS / appropriateness questions Q25-PM through Q30-PM). Schedule and tier caps are fixed at issuance in the Final Terms and not modifiable mid-life.
Final Settlement (Dated PM Series only)At Final Resolution Time T*: trading flips reduce-only at T*−1h (no new positions or transfers); expiry mark is the 30-minute TWAP of the Reference Price over [T*−30min, T*] (TWAP rather than last-print mitigates manipulation in the thin pre-resolution window); cash settlement value per cert is max(0, (expiry_TWAP − B_current) × N_current) for LONG YES, with sign reversal for SHORT YES, where B_current and N_current are the Series parameters following the final (T*−24h) Calendar Restrike Event; cash distribution in the cert's settlement currency to all holders on the snapshot block immediately before T*; Series permanently retired post-settlement. No automatic rollover — a successor Series referencing a later T* (e.g., next election cycle) may be listed in advance under supplementary Final Terms but holders must opt in by purchasing the successor Series.

What is materially novel about this programme Four product features sit at or just outside the boundary of established structured-product practice. (a) Non-financial-instrument underlying: the Reference Price is a real-world event probability quote, not an asset price — this is the programme's defining structural feature and the most novel. The cat-bond precedent (event-linked notes treated as transferable securities) is the closest TradFi analogue; the question is whether the precedent extends to actively-quoted, continuously-priced PM venues. See Section S9-PM and Question 21-PM. (b) Continuous-probability-path payoff that avoids the ESMA binary-options ban while the underlying terminal state is binary — the cert trades at intermediate values throughout its life, terminates either by KO Event or at T* via TWAP of the venue probability, and (for dated Series) deleverages to spot-equivalent before terminal. See Section S9-PM, Question 28-PM. (c) NAV-preserving Calendar Restrike on the same security token as the programme-principal mechanic for dated Series — pre-disclosed, deterministic, in-place parameter update (Barrier and Contract Size), not a token swap or burn-and-reissue. The tax characterisation (does this constitute a "disposal"?) is the load-bearing legal question for the dated format. See Question 25-PM. (d) Polymarket-on-chain Reference Price and TWAP computation for selected Series — the Reference Price is read from a non-EU, non-regulated, fully on-chain venue, and the TWAP can be computed in-band by the smart contract. BMR posture and benchmark-administrator characterisation are both open. See Question 29-PM.

The KO architecture itself, the hedge model, the MMB calibration, the add-margin mechanism, the security-token format, the SPV election, the qualified-custodian integration, and the transferability principle are not novel — they are imported unchanged from the multi-asset programme, which builds on 20+ years of German Turbo / KO certificate precedent.

Key Formulas — Programme-Level (PM-only)

The deterministic formulas published in the PM-only base prospectus. Per-Series parameters (Format election, Reference Venue, Event identifier, T* for dated Series, Calendar Deleveraging Schedule for dated Series, Contract Size, Barrier at issuance, MMB%, Carry Settlement cadence) are fixed in the Final Terms. Probability quotes denoted in cents (0¢–100¢); a contract paying $1 at YES resolution has the venue probability as its mark.

F-Value. Certificate Value

The value of a Prediction-Market Knock-Out Certificate at any point in time is:

Long YES certificate: Certificate Value = (Reference Price − Knock-Out Barrier) × Contract Size Short YES certificate (= Long NO at complementary probability): Certificate Value = (Knock-Out Barrier − Reference Price) × Contract Size Where: Reference Price = the Series' Reference Venue's continuously-published probability quote (cents) for the identified event, per §3.1quinquies of ko-cert-primer.html as applied to the PM-only programme. Kalshi: venue mid-price or last traded (per Final Terms). Polymarket: venue smart- contract mid-price or block-window VWAP (per Final Terms). Knock-Out Barrier = fixed at issuance per Series; adjusted at each Carry Settlement per F-Carry; additionally restruck at each scheduled Calendar Restrike Event per F-Restrike for dated Series. Contract Size = multiplier in cash units per cent of probability (fixed at issuance; updated by F-Restrike for dated Series). E.g., Contract Size = $1,000 / ¢ means one certificate gains/loses $1,000 for every 1¢ move in the probability quote between Barrier and Reference Price.

F-Lev. Implied Leverage and PnL

Implied Leverage (current, live) = Reference Price ÷ (Reference Price − Barrier) PnL per 1¢ move in probability = ± Contract Size × 1¢ Worked example (long YES on US election, Reference Price 47¢, Barrier 37.6¢, Contract Size $1,000 / ¢): Certificate Value = (47 − 37.6) × $1,000 = $9,400 Implied Leverage = 47 / (47 − 37.6) = 5.0× PnL per 1¢ move = $1,000 × 1¢ = $1,000 per certificate Worked example (long YES on Fed-cut-Dec2026, Reference Price 78¢, Barrier 70.2¢, Contract Size $500 / ¢): Certificate Value = (78 − 70.2) × $500 = $3,900 Implied Leverage = 78 / (78 − 70.2) = 10.0× PnL per 1¢ move = $500 × 1¢ = $500 per certificate

Implied leverage drifts continuously as the Reference Price moves — identical in form to a perpetual-future position on a financial-instrument underlying. The Series identifier encodes the Barrier (in cents), not the leverage (see F-Naming).

F-Carry. Barrier Adjustment — Carry Passthrough

At each Carry Settlement of the Series, the Barrier is adjusted to exactly mirror the carry cost (or credit) the issuer pays (or receives) on the hedge for that Series. For PM Series, continuous carry is typically zero — there is no funding-rate analogue on Kalshi or Polymarket. Any platform fee charged by the Reference Venue on the issuer's hedge position is the only non-zero carry component and is typically aggregated weekly (perpetual Series) or to final settlement (dated Series).

At each scheduled Carry Settlement (default cadence — perpetual PM Series: weekly; dated PM Series: aggregated to T* unless Final Terms specify a different cadence; cadence set per-Series in Final Terms): For long YES certificates: New Barrier = Previous Barrier + (Carry Rate × Reference Price at settlement) For short YES certificates: New Barrier = Previous Barrier − (Carry Rate × Reference Price at settlement) Where Carry Rate is: PM Series (default) → 0 (no funding-rate analogue on Kalshi / Polymarket) PM Series w/ venue fee → venue platform fee component on the issuer's hedge, expressed as a proportional rate (typically <1bp / week for perpetual Series; bundled to settlement for dated Series) Sign convention: positive Carry Rate = longs pay shorts (the typical case). For PM Series the Carry Rate is in practice zero or de minimis; the F-Carry mechanism is retained in the prospectus for definitional completeness and parity with the multi-asset programme.

F-Restrike. Calendar Restrike — Dated PM Series (programme-principal mechanic)

Applies only to dated PM Series with a Calendar Deleveraging Schedule (Term Sheet row above). At each pre-disclosed timestamp in the Schedule, the Series undergoes an in-place NAV-preserving Calendar Restrike Event. The Reference Price M_t used in the computation is a 30-minute TWAP of the Reference Price ending at the scheduled timestamp (same anti-manipulation methodology as the final-settlement TWAP at T*); this prevents single-block mark manipulation around the restrike instant. The mechanic is implemented as an in-place parameter update on the same security token — Barrier and Contract Size are updated by the formula below; the holder's token balance, ISIN, and contract address are unchanged.

At each scheduled Calendar Restrike Event for a long YES dated PM Series: M_t = 30-min TWAP of Reference Price ending at restrike timestamp B_old = Barrier of pre-restrike Series N_old = Contract Size (multiplier) of pre-restrike Series L_new = target leverage cap of post-restrike Series (per the Schedule) V_t = (M_t − B_old) × N_old (cert NAV at restrike, pre-event) Compute new Series parameters: B_new = M_t × (1 − 1 / L_new) (new Barrier — moves further from M_t) N_new = V_t / (M_t − B_new) (new Contract Size — preserves NAV) Post-restrike at mark M_t: Cert NAV = V_t (preserved by construction) Implied forward leverage = L_new (by construction) For short YES dated PM Series: swap (M_t − B) for (B − M_t). Holder's token balance: unchanged ISIN / contract address: unchanged Series identifier on-chain: parameter-update event emitted; token address constant; same TVTG / sui-generis security throughout the life of the Series The event is an in-place parameter update on the same security (NOT a token swap, NOT a burn-and-reissue, NOT a new ISIN issuance). Worked example — PM-KALSHI-USELECT2028-LONG-KO38-NOV2028, 5× at issuance: At issuance (T*−2y), M = 47¢: B = 47 × (1 − 1/5) = 37.6¢ N = $1,000 / ¢ (multiplier) NAV/cert = (47 − 37.6) × 1,000 = $9,400 Implied lev = 47 / (47 − 37.6) = 5.0× At T*−30d, M_t (TWAP) = 51¢, L_new = 3.0×: V_t = (51 − 37.6) × 1,000 = $13,400 B_new = 51 × (1 − 1/3) = 34.0¢ N_new = 13,400 / (51 − 34) = $788.2 / ¢ NAV/cert post-restrike = $13,400 (preserved) Implied forward leverage = 3.0× (by construction) At T*−7d, M_t = 56¢, L_new = 2.0×: V_t = (56 − 34.0) × 788.2 = $17,341 B_new = 56 × (1 − 1/2) = 28.0¢ N_new = 17,341 / (56 − 28) = $619.3 / ¢ NAV/cert post-restrike = $17,341 Implied forward leverage = 2.0× At T*−24h, M_t = 62¢, L_new = 1.0×: V_t = (62 − 28) × 619.3 = $21,056 B_new = 62 × (1 − 1/1) = 0.0¢ (barrier collapses to floor) N_new = 21,056 / (62 − 0) = $339.6 / ¢ NAV/cert post-restrike = $21,056 Implied forward leverage = 1.0× (spot-equivalent into terminal) At T*, trading flips reduce-only at T*−1h; final settlement at TWAP M_T* (see F-Settle below).

The mechanic mirrors the carry-passthrough Barrier Adjustment (F-Carry) in form (Barrier and Contract Size are updated by deterministic formula at a scheduled cadence) but on a separate calendar axis. The cadence is per-Series and pre-disclosed in the Final Terms at issuance; the issuer correspondingly unwinds a proportional fraction of the hedge book at each scheduled restrike (slippage absorbed into the issuer spread; pre-funded in the issuance pricing). See pm-cert-calendar-deleveraging-supplement.md for the full mechanic, hedge-book unwind handling, smart-contract implementation outline, oracle / TWAP-source treatment, idempotency / grace-window handling on oracle stalls, and the open tax / PRIIPS / appropriateness questions (Q25-PM through Q30-PM below).

F-KO. Knock-Out Event — Residual Value (Standard Calibration)

If the Reference Price equals or breaches the current Barrier (for long YES: from above; for short YES: from below) before T* (for dated Series) or at any time (for perpetual Series), the Series terminates immediately. Holder receives:

Standard Calibration: Residual Value = max(0, (Reference Price at KO − Barrier at KO) × Contract Size) (short YES Series: signs reversed) In practice, for highly leveraged Series under Standard Calibration, the residual on a clean barrier-hit is zero or near-zero. The optional MMB Calibration (F-MMB) is the recommended default for retail PM Series so that the holder receives a deterministic non-zero residual on KO, matching the user experience of a perp liquidation.

F-MMB. Knock-Out Event — MMB Calibration (Perp-Parity)

The optional MMB Calibration brings the certificate's user experience into exact parity with perp liquidation mechanics: the Barrier is set at the point of zero-equity, and the holder additionally receives a fixed Maintenance Margin Buffer (MMB) cash payment on KO — mirroring how a perp exchange returns the maintenance margin remnant on liquidation.

MMB Calibration: Barrier at issuance = Reference Price × (1 − 1/Leverage) (zero-equity barrier — same algebraic relationship as Standard Calibration) Series Notional Value = Reference Price at issuance × Contract Size MMB Amount = MMB% × Series Notional Value (typical MMB% = 0.3% to 1.0%, fixed per Series) At KO event: Residual Value to holder = max(0, (Reference Price at KO − Barrier at KO) × Contract Size) + MMB Amount Worked example: 5× long YES on US election; Reference Price 47¢; Barrier 37.6¢; Contract Size $1,000 / ¢; MMB% 0.5%: Notional Value = 47¢ × $1,000 / ¢ = $47,000 MMB Amount = 0.5% × $47,000 = $235 per certificate Standard Calibration residual on KO (clean barrier hit at 37.6¢): max(0, (37.6 − 37.6) × 1,000) = $0 MMB Calibration residual on KO (clean barrier hit at 37.6¢): max(0, (37.6 − 37.6) × 1,000) + $235 = $235 MMB Amount is the deterministic floor on the residual value at KO. Convention for dated PM Series at final Calendar Restrike Event (T*−24h, leverage cap 1.0×): the MMB is set to zero following the T*−24h restrike since the Series at that point holds spot-equivalent exposure and the MMB rationale (returning a maintenance buffer at forced liquidation) is moot. Counsel input requested on this convention — see Question 15-PM(b).

F-Settle. Final Settlement at T* — Dated PM Series

Applies only to dated PM Series, at the Series' Final Resolution Time T* identified in the Final Terms. Mechanics mirror the trading halt + TWAP expiry mark used by regulated exchange-traded option settlements (e.g., CME monthly equity-option SOQ) and by leading PM venues (Kalshi's resolution rules, Polymarket's UMA-oracle resolution).

Sequence: T* − 1h Trading halt — flips to reduce-only window (no new positions, no transfers in or out, no add-margin top-ups). T* − 30min TWAP window opens. T* Final Resolution Time. TWAP window closes. Expiry mark = 30-min TWAP of Reference Price over [T* − 30min, T*] (TWAP rather than last-print mitigates manipulation in the thin pre-resolution window). Settlement value per certificate: Long YES: V_final = max(0, (TWAP_expiry − B_current) × N_current) Short YES: V_final = max(0, (B_current − TWAP_expiry) × N_current) where B_current and N_current are the Series parameters following the final (T*−24h) Calendar Restrike Event. Cash distribution: in the Series' settlement currency (USD / EUR / USDC per Final Terms) to all holders on the snapshot block immediately before T*. Distribution settles within the standard cycle for the settlement currency (USD wire / EUR SEPA / USDC on-chain on Polygon or the SPV's elected chain). Series retirement: smart-contract state preserved for historical query; Series identifier permanently retired; no automatic rollover (a successor Series referencing a later T* may be listed under supplementary Final Terms; holders must opt in). Fallback on cancelled / postponed / contested resolution: per Final Terms, default to (a) postponement of T* matching the venue's postponement of resolution, with trading remaining reduce-only; (b) Series Termination at Fair Value Payment if the venue's resolution mechanism fails terminally (e.g., venue insolvency, permanent oracle failure). See Question 21-PM(c).

F-Settle is structurally a one-off application of F-KO with the Barrier-at-T*−24h-restrike substituted into the residual-value formula and computed against the TWAP rather than a single mark. The settlement value can equivalently be expressed as: V_final = max(0, residual_at_TWAP_expiry) using the F-KO arithmetic. The two are presented separately because (i) F-Settle is the modal exit for dated PM Series under normal resolution (rather than F-KO), and (ii) the TWAP construction is a specific anti-manipulation mechanism that distinguishes F-Settle from a same-block KO.

F-AddMargin. Add-Margin Mechanism

Holders may deposit additional collateral to move the Barrier further from the Reference Price — reducing implied leverage and reducing the probability of KO. The issuer simultaneously deposits matching margin into the hedge so the hedge alignment is preserved. Imported unchanged from the multi-asset programme; see Question 6-PM on CFD-reclassification analysis.

Top-up of ΔC (in settlement currency) by holder (long YES Series): New Barrier = Previous Barrier − (ΔC / Contract Size / Number of Certificates) Implied Leverage falls accordingly; KO probability falls. The mechanism is the only top-up mechanism offered; no continuous margin management, partial close-out, or cross-margining. For Dated PM Series, add-margin top-ups are permitted at any time before the T*−1h reduce-only window, including between Calendar Restrike Events. Each Restrike Event subsequently recomputes the new Barrier and Contract Size from the post-top-up state.

S1-PM. Prediction Markets as the Underlying — Non-Financial-Instrument Event Probability

The defining structural feature of this programme is that the Reference Price is not the price of a financial instrument or commodity — it is a continuously-published probability quote (in cents; 0¢–100¢) for the resolution of a real-world event, published by a prediction-market venue. Major venues: Kalshi (CFTC-regulated US event-contract exchange) and Polymarket (on-chain prediction market on Polygon). They trade binary outcome contracts: "Will X happen by date Y?" — pays $1 if YES, $0 if NO; the continuous market price is the venue's quoted probability.

Two distinct questions arise: (i) can a transferable-security product reference a prediction-market venue as Reference Price, when the underlying is a non-financial-instrument event probability? (ii) does the non-binary, continuous-probability-path structure of the KO Certificate avoid the ESMA binary-options ban (Decision EU 2018/795)?

S3-PM

S3-PM. Calendar Deleveraging Schedule — Programme-Principal Mechanic for Dated Series

The Calendar Deleveraging Schedule is the structural mechanism that makes the Dated PM KO format work. Without it, a 5× long PM cert held into resolution would experience a near-instantaneous −100% loss on the wrong side of resolution at the moment the venue probability snaps from intermediate value to 0¢; with it, the holder's exposure is mechanically reduced to 1× in the 24 hours before T*, so the terminal jump produces a loss consistent with the holder's terminal risk budget rather than an outsized loss inconsistent with their issuance risk budget.

Our working position. The Calendar Restrike Event is an in-place parameter update on the same security token. The holder's token balance, ISIN, and contract address are unchanged; the Series identifier on-chain emits a parameter-update event; the Barrier and Contract Size are updated by the deterministic F-Restrike formula computed at the 30-min TWAP of the Reference Price ending at the restrike timestamp. The mechanic is mathematically equivalent (in form) to the F-Carry barrier adjustment but on a separate calendar axis. The cadence is pre-disclosed in the Final Terms at issuance and not modifiable mid-life.

Why this approach instead of a token swap or burn-and-reissue. Two reasons. Tax characterisation — an in-place parameter adjustment under a pre-disclosed schedule is far more defensible as a non-realising event for the holder than a token-for-token exchange. Custody and broker integration — most qualified crypto custodians (Tangany, HAL, BvdH) and MiFID-licensed brokers handle parameter updates as a corporate-action data feed; they do not handle automatic token swaps cleanly across the board.

Load-bearing legal question — Q25-PM. If the Calendar Restrike Event is characterised as a "disposal" under DE EStG §20, AT KESt, or LI VStG, the dated PM Series becomes economically unworkable for retail (every restrike crystallises capital gain), and we would need to either redesign the dated format around the tax characterisation or shift dated Series to professional-only distribution. Counsel input on Q25-PM, separately for DE, AT, and FL/CH, is the highest-priority response from the engagement.

S6-PM. Transferable Token Model — Ondo / xStocks Approach (Tokens Distributable w/o KYC)

The intended transfer model follows the approach pioneered by Ondo Finance (USDY, OUSG) and xStocks (tokenized equities): KYC is required to receive tokens directly from the issuer; once in circulation, tokens may be freely transferred between any wallet addresses on-chain — including unregistered wallets — without per-transfer KYC. The restriction applies at the point of issuance (issuer → user), not at the point of secondary transfer (user → user). This is the programme-level "tokens distributable w/o KYC" principle in the Term Sheet. Imported unchanged from the multi-asset programme.

Transfer restriction model — issuer-gated at issuance, free in secondary: Issuer → User: KYC required. Only KYC-verified wallet addresses may receive tokens directly from the issuer (primary issuance / primary-market purchase). Smart contract enforces: issuer mint/transfer only to whitelisted addresses. User → User (secondary market): Transfers PERMITTED to any wallet address, including non-whitelisted. No smart contract restriction on peer-to-peer secondary transfers. The token is freely transferable once it leaves the issuer. Compliance maintained via: - KYC at primary issuance (AML/CFT check at point of first sale) - Issuer can freeze/blacklist specific addresses (sanctions compliance) - On-chain transfer history is publicly auditable - No ongoing KYC obligation for secondary-market transfers This mirrors how bearer securities work in traditional finance: Bearer bond issued to identified first holder; subsequent transfers between third parties are not gatekept by the issuer.

This model maximises secondary-market liquidity and concentrates the compliance burden at the issuance gate (the point the issuer controls). Ondo Finance has operated this model since 2023 across multiple jurisdictions without regulatory intervention. The key question is whether EU AML/CFT (AMLD6, MiCA, Transfer of Funds Regulation) imposes stricter obligations on secondary transfers than the US framework under which the Ondo precedent developed.

PM-specific consideration — sanctions reach. A PM cert referencing a politically-sensitive event (e.g., sanctions-list elections, head-of-state continuation, ceasefire events) may attract sanctions concerns specifically — i.e., the cert is the wrong side of a sanctions-driven freeze if the underlying event is itself sanctions-relevant. Counsel input requested on whether the issuer's freeze/seize function must reach cert holders based on the cert's underlying event, or only on the cert holder's wallet address being separately sanctioned.

S8. Token-Only Issuance — CFD Reclassification Risk?

For crypto specifically, if ESMA were to review an unlisted, OTC-traded leveraged token with crypto exposure, there is a non-zero risk of scrutiny of the transferable-security classification — and, if reclassified, the 2× retail crypto-CFD cap would bite.
