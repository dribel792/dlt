Executive Summary

Spot crypto demand is soft. Retail volumes are off the 2024 highs, directional speculation is muted, and the MiCAR rulebook has reshuffled the European landscape. That backdrop has not weakened DLT's position — it has clarified it.

Two opportunities are driving the next 12 months: B2B2X — a wave of newly licensed MiCAR institutions looking for the next product line they can offer their clients under MiFID; and B2B — large institutions whose crypto trading desks are still operating well below full capacity, keeping ECN-based liquidity provision and best execution in demand.

DLT will build out three new product lines on top of the existing MiFID derivatives backbone — leverage, TradFi, and yield — so that we always have a product that fits the market regime. Direction strong → leverage (perps, sprinters, 0DTE) sells. Direction dull but equities working → TradFi (equity perps, indices, commodities) sells. Direction flat → yield (dual investment, shark fin, BRC, capital-protected notes) sells. The infrastructure is the same; only the configuration changes. That is the bet we press for the next four quarters.

Where the crypto market is right now

A softer directional market, but two structural openings — one regulatory, one institutional.

The crypto market in mid-2026 is in a regime shift. Spot volumes across European retail venues are materially below their 2024 peak. Funding rates on major perps have compressed. Memes and high-beta narratives are recycling, but without the capital inflows of the prior cycle. For brokers and exchanges with a pure directional-crypto product shelf, this is a difficult year.

The underlying structure of the market, however, has improved in two ways that are directly relevant to DLT:

04

Equity perpetuals — the natural extension

Perp mechanics applied to single names and indices: what crypto users want when crypto is flat.

The retail user who has spent two years trading BTC and ETH perps already understands funding, mark price, liquidation, and leverage. The mechanics translate one-for-one to single-name equity perpetuals: NVDA-PERP, TSLA-PERP, SPX-PERP, GOLD-PERP. Same UX, same risk engine, same liquidation logic — just a different underlying.

This is exactly the product retail wants in a flat-crypto market. They keep the muscle memory and the leverage; the underlying gives them something to trade. For the partner platform, it is incremental product without rebuilding a single front-end component.

24/7 markets — the structural differentiator

Cash equities trade ~6.5h a day; futures markets close on weekends; the underlying venues stop. Equity perpetuals do not — they are 24/7 markets. The mark price keeps moving against an index reference, funding accrues continuously, and the order book stays open through nights, weekends and holidays. That gives the partner platform a product that is always tradeable — exactly when retail users actually open the app — and gives DLT a regulated wrapper around something traditional brokers structurally cannot offer.

Why it's a clean MiFID product

Equity perpetuals can be structured as either (a) derivatives referencing a regulated underlying (cleanest), or (b) securitised perpetuals — factor certificates / mini-futures issued under an EU prospectus and distributed OTC through licensed brokers. DLT covers both paths.

Why partners want it now

Crypto-native venues cannot offer equity exposure without a securities licence. DLT's MiFID stack means a MiCAR platform can offer NVDA, TSLA, SPX exposure to their existing client base on day one — without becoming an investment firm themselves.

05

Yield strategies

Packaged option strategies that retail can buy in one click — the dominant retail product when direction is sideways.

In a flat market, retail does not stop wanting product — it stops wanting directional product. The dominant retail flow in Asia and in European structured products in 2026 is into yield-enhanced structured strategies: pre-packaged option positions that pay an attractive coupon in exchange for capped upside, conditional downside, or a barrier corridor.

DLT's MiFID backbone supports the four dominant retail yield formats — Dual Investment, Shark Fin, BRC (Barrier Reverse Convertibles), and Principal-Protected Notes. Each is explained below with payoff profile, indicative yield, and how the downside is protected.

Yield Product Indicative APR Term Downside Protection Worst-case for user
Dual Investment 15 – 60% p.a. 1 – 14 days None — directional conversion at strike Converted into the other asset at the strike (sold above market or bought below)
Shark Fin 2% (base) → up to 30% p.a. (fin peak) 1 – 3 months 100% capital protected Receive principal back plus the base APR (no upside)
BRC (Reverse Convertible) 8 – 25% p.a. (fixed coupon) 3 – 12 months Conditional — barrier (typically 60–80% of spot) If barrier breached, redeemed in the underlying at the strike (loss vs. cash)
Principal-Protected Note 0% guaranteed + capped upside participation 6 – 24 months 100% capital protected at maturity Receive principal back with zero (or low) upside if underlying did not perform

5.1 · Dual Investment / Covered Calls

What it is. The user deposits an asset (e.g. BTC or USDC) and commits to either sell at a higher strike if the underlying rallies, or buy at a lower strike if it dips. In exchange, they earn a fixed, attractive yield (often 15–60% annualised, scaled to the term) for the duration of the product, typically 1–14 days.

How it works under the hood. The product sells an out-of-the-money option (a call if the user deposited the asset; a put if they deposited the quote currency). The premium received is the yield paid to the user. At expiry, if the option is out of the money, the user gets their deposit back plus the yield. If the option is in the money, the user is "settled" into the other asset at the strike — they have effectively sold high or bought low.

Covered Call / Dual Investment payoff — flat yield in the expected range, conversion above the strike.

Why retail loves it. Headline yields look high, the worst case is framed as "you sold your BTC at a higher price than today" or "you bought BTC at a cheaper price than today" — both feel like wins. Binance, OKX, Bybit have made this one of the highest-volume retail products globally.

5.2 · Shark Fin

What it is. A capital-protected structured note that pays a directional, range-linked yield. The user picks a direction (bull or bear); the product defines a starting price and a knock-out barrier. As long as the underlying moves in their direction but does not breach the barrier, the APR rises linearly with the move. If the underlying retraces, the user receives a small base APR. If the barrier is breached, the product knocks out and only the base APR is paid. Principal is always returned at maturity.

How it works under the hood. A zero-coupon bond delivers principal protection; the residual premium funds an up-and-out call spread (for a bull fin) or a down-and-out put spread (for a bear fin). The strike sits at spot, the knock-out sits at the upper (or lower) barrier. Payoff between strike and barrier is linear in the underlying — that is the "fin" shape, and it is exactly how Bitget, OKX and Binance display the product to retail.

Bull Shark Fin (Bitget-style) — base APR below spot, linearly rising APR up to the barrier, cliff back to base APR if knocked out.

Why retail loves it. "Capital protected" is the strongest selling angle in any structured product. Pair that with double-digit headline coupons and this is the dominant European retail format outside of pure crypto. Translating it to BTC/ETH/SPX underlyings is what makes DLT's stack uniquely positioned.

5.3 · BRC — Barrier Reverse Convertibles

What it is. A yield-enhanced note that pays a fixed coupon regardless of market direction, with a conditional downside: if the underlying breaches a pre-set barrier (typically 60–80% of spot) during the life of the product, the user is redeemed in the underlying at the strike at maturity rather than in cash. If the barrier is never touched, the user receives full principal plus the coupon.

How it works under the hood. A zero-coupon bond + short down-and-in put. The premium received from selling the barrier put funds the enhanced coupon. The buyer is effectively being paid to take conditional downside exposure below the barrier.

Indicative terms. 8–25% p.a. fixed coupon, 3–12 month tenor, barrier at 60–80% of spot. Classic European retail format — by far the highest-volume structured product category in Switzerland, Germany and the Nordics. Crypto-linked BRCs on BTC and ETH are the cleanest first translation to a MiCAR-platform audience.

5.4 · Principal-Protected Notes

What it is. A note that guarantees 100% return of capital at maturity plus a participation in any upside of the underlying. The user gives up the risk-free interest they would have earned on a deposit; in exchange they get exposure to the upside of BTC, ETH, SPX, gold — with zero downside risk beyond opportunity cost.

How it works under the hood. A zero-coupon bond is bought at a discount; the discount funds an at-the-money call (or call spread) on the underlying. At maturity, the zero pays back par (= principal protection); the call pays the upside. The participation rate (often 40–80%) and any cap are tuned to the level of interest rates and option volatility at issuance.

Indicative terms. 6–24 month tenor, 100% capital protection, 40–80% participation in the underlying, optional cap. The most regulator-friendly structured format that exists — and the easiest pitch to a partner platform whose clients are reluctant to take direct crypto downside.

Why these four matter for DLT

Dual Investment, Shark Fin, BRC and Principal-Protected Notes between them cover the full retail risk spectrum — from "I'll take conversion risk for 30% yield" all the way to "I want zero downside but exposure to the upside." That spectrum is exactly what a partner platform needs to retain a diverse client base: a yield product for every risk tolerance, all sourced from one MiFID counterparty.

These products also lean into the same gamification loop that has driven crypto retail volumes for the last cycle: short tenors, clear payoff diagrams, headline APRs, one-click subscription, daily settlement. They cater to different risk profiles and to gamified, engagement-driven retail behaviour — the same users who trade perps will subscribe to dual investments and shark fins between trades. For the partner platform, this is what keeps the app open seven days a week.

Leverage products

Securitised and option-based leverage formats — what retail buys when they want a directional bet, not a yield.

Yield products are what retail buys when direction is flat. Leverage products are what they buy when they want to take a direction. DLT's MiFID stack supports both formats that dominate the European leveraged-retail market: 0DTE options for short-tenor, low-premium speculation; and securitised knock-out certificates (sprinters / turbos / factor certificates / mini-futures) for the traditional German / Swiss / Nordic retail audience.

Leverage Product Max Loss Tenor Funding / Roll Cost Audience

6.1 · 0DTE — Zero-Day-To-Expiry options

What it is. Options that expire on the same trading day they are bought. The user picks a strike and a direction, pays a small premium, and either captures a multiple or loses the premium by end of session.

0DTE has been the single fastest-growing product on US options exchanges. SPX 0DTE now exceeds 50% of total SPX option volume on some sessions. The mechanic — pay a small fixed premium, get binary-style exposure to a one-day move — translates directly to crypto and to single-name equity perpetual references.

0DTE call payoff — small fixed cost, leveraged exposure to a single-session move.

Why it matters for DLT. 0DTE is a high-frequency, high-engagement product. It keeps users on platform daily without requiring directional conviction from them. For the partner platform, it dramatically improves session frequency metrics. For DLT, it is incremental order flow into the derivatives stack we already operate.

6.2 · Sprinters / Knock-Out certificates

What it is. A leveraged participation certificate with an automatic knock-out barrier. The user takes a directional view with embedded leverage (5x–50x). If the underlying moves in their direction, the certificate tracks the move on a leveraged basis. If the underlying touches a pre-defined barrier, the certificate knocks out and is settled at (or near) zero.

How it differs from a perp. A perp is a continuously rolling, cash-settled futures contract with funding payments. A sprinter is a securitised instrument — a certificate issued under an EU prospectus with an ISIN, distributed OTC through MiFID-licensed brokers. The leverage and knock-out are baked into the structure. There is no funding payment and no margin call: maximum loss is capped at the premium paid.

Long Sprinter / Knock-Out certificate — leveraged participation until barrier; instant knock-out below barrier.

Why retail loves it. Sprinters are the dominant retail leveraged product in Germany, Austria, Switzerland and the Nordics — total volume across European exchanges runs into hundreds of billions of EUR per year. The product is familiar, accepted by regulators, and well-understood. Putting BTC, ETH, SPX, single-name equity sprinters in front of a retail MiCAR client base is a clean product extension.

B2B — liquidity provision & best execution

The institutional side. Below-capacity tier-1 desks plus ECN distribution keeps this business durable.

The institutional half of the market tells a different story than the retail half. Large bank trading desks have opened crypto lines but are nowhere near full deployment. They cannot yet warehouse meaningful crypto risk, their internal mandates remain conservative, and many do not have direct exchange relationships. That keeps the demand for external liquidity provision and routed best execution structurally elevated.

8.1 · ECN distribution remains the dominant pipe

For institutional crypto flow, three ECNs dominate the market: 360T, oneZero, and Crossover Markets. Every meaningful institutional liquidity programme — including ours — runs through one or more of these. DLT is connected and live on all three.

360T

Deutsche Börse-owned multi-asset venue with the deepest bank connectivity. The institutional default for crypto NDFs and structured flow.

oneZero

Broker-focused infrastructure layer with strong retail-broker connectivity. Where flow from FX-style brokers crosses into the crypto market.

Crossover Markets

Institutional ECN purpose-built for digital assets. Smaller than 360T but with strong relationships in funds & market-makers.

8.2 · Best execution as a moat

MiFID II best-execution obligations are not a checkbox for institutional counterparties — they are an audit requirement. The institutions that can demonstrate routed best execution across multiple venues, with measurable reporting, win the mandates. The institutions that route to a single venue do not. DLT's documented, multi-venue routing is one of the few defensible institutional moats in the European crypto market today.

The point

The B2B side is not a growth story — it is a stability story. ECN flow plus best-execution mandates produce a stable institutional revenue base while the B2B2X retail side is where the upside lives. We hold both.
