Slide 3 · The Shelf2026 | Confidential
DLT Finance · Plain English

Eight words you will hear in the next ten minutes

DLT Finance
Wrapper
The legal packaging around a bet. The same economic exposure can be sold as a derivative contract, or wrapped as a security — the payoff is identical, but who can buy it and who must be licensed changes completely.
ISIN
The twelve-character identifier every tradable security carries. If a product has one, an ordinary broker can list it, settle it and report it using the plumbing they already run. No ISIN, no shelf space.
ETPExchange-traded product
A security that tracks something else — an index, a coin, a leveraged position. Turbos, knock-out certificates and mini futures are all ETPs. You buy it like a share; you can never lose more than you paid.
PerpPerpetual future
A leveraged bet on a price with no expiry date. It stays anchored to the real price through a small payment passed between buyers and sellers, so nobody has to manage a settlement calendar.
Structured product
A package: most of the money goes somewhere safe that earns interest, a small slice buys an option. Change the mix and you change the payoff. That is the whole idea.
Delta-1
Anything that moves one-for-one with the underlying price — spot, a tracker, a tokenised share. No leverage, no optionality. The plain version of exposure.
B-book
Taking the other side of a client's trade rather than passing it to the market. Because most leveraged retail traders lose over time, being the counterparty is a revenue line — the reason issuing pays a multiple of distributing.
Counterparty
Whoever stands on the other side and owes the money. Distributing a product earns a commission; being the counterparty earns the economics. Everything in this deck turns on which of the two we are.
Standing point

DLT is the infrastructure that makes these products available to everyone — as the counterparty. Every term above is a way of describing that same sentence more precisely.

Structured products — first, what they actually are

DLT Finance
The whole category in four sentences

A structured product is a package. Most of the money goes somewhere safe that earns interest. A small slice buys an option. The safe part guarantees you get your money back; the option part is the upside. Change the mix and you change the payoff — that is the entire idea.

~90% kept safe, earning interestthis is what guarantees the capital comes back
~10%buys upside
Capital protectionThe bonus
1What does it do?example: shark fin

Your money back, guaranteed. Plus up to 8% if BTC stays inside a range. If it breaks out you still get your money back — you just lose the bonus.

One recipe among many — covered calls, protected notes and dual-currency are the same machinery with a different mix. The client never sees an option; they see a term, a range and two outcomes.

In range
You get your money back + the bonusthe best case, and the one most clients get
Breaks out
You get your money back + base interestyou lose the bonus, never the capital
2Why is it interesting?
Half a step ahead — and it holds the deposit

It is the only product on the shelf that locks a balance in place for one to three months. That money cannot leave for a competitor mid-term.

It also reaches a different customer entirely — the one who will never touch 30× leverage but will accept "capital back, plus maybe 8%". It turns a passive spot balance into a fee-earning one at zero extra acquisition cost.

Shark fin — what the client actually sees

DLT Finance
The payoff, drawnwhat you earn vs. where BTC ends up
The only thing that varies is the yield. The capital is returned at maturity in every single case.
The three things a client is told
How long is my money tied up?1–3 months
Can I lose my money?No — 100% returned at maturity
What do I earn?~2% floor, up to ~8% best case
Three outcomes, in plain language
Best
BTC drifts up and stays inside the rangeCapital back, plus the full bonus. This is the scenario the product is designed for.
Flat / down
BTC goes nowhere, or fallsCapital back, plus base interest. The client is no worse off than holding cash — and better off than holding BTC.
Breakout
BTC rips through the top of the rangeCapital back, plus base interest. The client gives up the bonus — the one real cost, and it is an opportunity cost, not a loss.
Why this is the example we use

It is the easiest structured product to explain to someone who has only ever held spot: you cannot lose your money, and here is what you give up in exchange. Every other recipe on the shelf is a variation on that trade-off.

What the four products add up to
  • Perps are the entry ticket. 12% of revenue, and we run them at agency margin — but without them we do not get the conversation. Offer them, do not fund them as a growth line.
  • ETPs are the business. 50% of revenue from 7% of users, because we issue rather than distribute. Everything about the licence and the risk engine exists to serve this line.
  • RWA is the growth. 20% of revenue, and the only line that grows by adding 104 trading days rather than more users — on a fixed cost base, against competitors who are closed.
  • Structured is the margin and the stickiness. 8% of revenue at the fattest margin, and the only product that locks the client's balance in place for months at a time.
  • Spot is acquisition. 100% of users, 8% of revenue, 200+ competitors. Defend it; do not invest to win it.
