0The one-page version

Positioning in one sentence
DLT is the regulated MiFID product layer of the group. We hand non-MiFID spot trading and trading ops to Uphold, and we run every MiFID product (perps, structured products, ETPs, tokenized securities) through one operating system: Haruko.
  • Get out of what costs us money: balance-sheet-heavy prop spot (inventory, LP collateral, manual settlement on the legacy platform) moves to Uphold through an API proxy.
  • Put our effort where the licence is the moat: perps as a commission broker, OTC structured products, securitized perps / ETPs, tokenized securities. Partners cannot do these without a MiFID entity.
  • One view of risk: every MiFID flow ends up in Haruko (OEMS + PMS). Until then, temporary solutions are fine as long as they can feed Haruko later. We don't build tooling that Haruko will replace.
  • Shortcuts are allowed, but they need an exit: every temporary solution in §8 has an exit criterion and an owner.
Exit / offload
Prop spot

Baader, Taiwan Mobile, YouHodler (soon). Proxy Uphold's API and settle from Uphold wallets. No inventory and no LP collateral on DLT's balance sheet.

Core MiFID
Perps · Structured · ETPs · Tokenized securities

Where DLT earns its margin. Best-ex for perps, Nexo as the first client for structured products and ETPs, Anduin as issuer and LP for securitized perps.

Infrastructure
Haruko

OEMS + PMS. Fully available in about 6 months, with flows migrated in stages. It becomes the single source of truth for risk, exposure and trading activity.

2Spot trading: four different flows

"Spot" at DLT covers four flows that work very differently. Don't treat them as one product. They differ in risk, settlement and future direction:

2.1 Prop spot (Baader, Taiwan Mobile, soon YouHodler) → move to Uphold

What it is. DLT acts as principal. It quotes clients, holds inventory, hedges with LPs and settles both legs. Of all our flows, this is the hardest to run:

  • Heavy post-trade work: settlement runs with every client and every LP, reconciliations, and fiat and crypto legs on different timelines.
  • Trapped cash: collateral sits with LPs to support credit lines, so the capital isn't working.
  • Inventory risk for little flow: we have to hold significant inventory relative to volume. When crypto falls for a month, that inventory produces negative PnL.
  • Legacy platform: execution runs on the old stack, so slippage and operational incidents happen.

Target model: proxy the Uphold API

1 · ClientSends an RFQ/order to DLT's existing API. The client-facing interface doesn't change.
→
2 · DLT proxyApplies the client's limits and spread/fee, then forwards the order to the Uphold API.
→
3 · UpholdExecutes from its own liquidity and inventory. DLT holds no inventory.
→
4 · SettlementCrypto moves directly Uphold wallet ↔ client wallet. DLT records and reconciles.

The result is that all trading and trading ops for this flow go to the parent company, and DLT can put its focus on MiFID products. This also removes most of our post-trade processes, which is the main reason this is Medium priority.

The open questions, answered as far as we can today

Q1 · Can settlement happen directly from Uphold custody?
Yes, technically. Uphold can send directly from its custody wallets to the client's whitelisted addresses and receive into them. Legally, the setup decides who is the client's counterparty, and there are two ways to set it up:
  • (a) DLT remains principal, Uphold settles on DLT's behalf. The client contract stays with DLT, and Uphold acts as DLT's execution and settlement provider. This is the least disruptive for clients, who keep their DLT contract. Under MiCA and KWG this is outsourcing of a material function. That means an outsourcing agreement, a notification to BaFin, and DLT keeping control and audit rights over Uphold's settlement process. The client's T&Cs/SSIs have to say that settlement may come from a third-party wallet operated on DLT's behalf.
  • (b) DLT becomes an introducer/broker and the client faces Uphold directly. This is cleaner for settlement, but each client needs a contract and KYB with the relevant Uphold entity, and DLT's revenue becomes a fee or rebate. That may be the right answer for some clients and not others.
Recommendation: model (a) as the default, because it keeps relationships and contracts intact, with model (b) available for clients who prefer to face Uphold. Legal/Compliance needs to confirm.
Q2 · If Uphold settles from its custody, does Uphold have Travel Rule obligations?
Yes, very likely. Under the EU Transfer of Funds Regulation (Reg. (EU) 2023/1113), Travel Rule duties sit with the CASP that carries out the transfer. When the coins leave Uphold-controlled custody, Uphold is the originator-side CASP. It has to send originator and beneficiary information, and it has to screen what it receives on inbound transfers. What follows from that:
  • We need to agree who is named as the originator: DLT (under model (a), DLT is Uphold's customer and the transfer is made for DLT) or the underlying client. Under (a), originator = DLT, beneficiary = the client/its custodian.
  • Find out which Uphold entity runs the wallets and what its regulatory status is (EU CASP under MiCA, or a non-EU entity). A non-EU sender into EU clients still triggers EU-side Travel Rule checks for the receiving CASP. US entities fall under the FinCEN Travel Rule (≥ USD 3,000).
  • Protocol interoperability: Baader, Taiwan Mobile and YouHodler have to be able to receive Travel Rule messages from Uphold's provider (Notabene / Sumsub / TRP / etc.). Check this per client early. It is a common reason go-lives slip.
  • For self-hosted client wallets above €1,000, there are proof-of-ownership requirements (signed message or Satoshi test). Uphold has to be able to perform or accept these.
Action: Compliance writes a one-page Travel Rule allocation memo (DLT vs Uphold) before the technical build starts.
Q3 · What does the migration look like?
  1. Legal & contracts: intra-group outsourcing/settlement agreement DLT ↔ Uphold; client T&C/SSI amendments; BaFin outsourcing notification.
  2. Travel Rule & KYB: Uphold set up as a known counterparty in each client's Travel Rule tool; each client completes KYB on Uphold where required.
  3. Wallet whitelisting: Uphold gives one set of deposit/withdrawal addresses per asset and chain. Each client whitelists them (most have a 24–72h cooling period, and some need dual approval). In return, clients' addresses are whitelisted at Uphold. Do proof-of-ownership tests per address.
  4. Fiat leg: decide where cash settles (DLT bank account vs Uphold account). This is often the forgotten part and should be decided first.
  5. Tech: build the proxy (order translation, client limits, markup, error mapping), a settlement instruction feed from Uphold, and a reconciliation job (Uphold fills ↔ client confirms ↔ on-chain transfers).
  6. Parallel run: 2–4 weeks per client. Small tickets on the new path, everything else on legacy. Run daily three-way reconciliation.
  7. Cut-over, client by client: notify the SSI change with notice, switch routing, freeze the old addresses after the last settlement.
  8. Unwind: sell down or transfer DLT inventory to Uphold, recall collateral from LPs, close LP accounts that are no longer needed, and decommission the legacy spot settlement jobs.
Suggested order: YouHodler first, onboarded straight onto the new model so we never build legacy settlement for them. Then Taiwan Mobile, then Baader. Baader goes last because its process is the most mature and the most regulated, so we should change it once the model is proven.
Shortcut / temporary solution Until the proxy is live: stop adding inventory. Hedge each client trade back-to-back with an LP straight away, rather than warehousing. That stops most of the negative-PnL bleed in down months without waiting for the migration. A second option, if the settlement agreement takes longer: execution via Uphold, settlement still from DLT wallets, pre-funded by Uphold. It's less clean, but it takes inventory risk off the book early.

2.2 Hedge-first spot for Uphold (client liquidations)

What it is. Uphold uses the DLT hedging engine to liquidate its clients' positions. This happens rarely, but when it does it is usually in volatile markets. The rule: DLT hedges first with an LP, gets an execution price, and only then fills Uphold's order at that price. DLT should never be left holding the position.

Execution logic (spec for the engine)

1 · ReceiveUphold sell order (asset, qty, optional limit, urgency/max duration).
→
2 · HedgeSend an IOC limit to primary LP at mid ± max slippage band.
→
3 · Partial?Re-send the remainder with max slippage to the same or an alternative venue (ranked by depth/price).
→
4 · Still open?Switch to a TWAP/VWAP schedule over the allowed window.
→
5 · Fill UpholdAt the VWAP of the hedge fills (+ agreed fee). Report any remainder that wasn't filled.
ParameterMeaningNeeds agreement with Uphold
max_slippage_bpsBand around reference mid per child orderYes, per asset and per volatility regime
venue_ladderOrdered list of LPs/venues to retry onNo (DLT internal)
max_retries / retry_intervalHow often to re-send before switching to algoNo
algo_mode, algo_windowTWAP or VWAP, and the maximum durationYes. Uphold must accept that a fill can take minutes
remainder_policyWhat to do if the order still isn't fully filled: return the partial, or keep working until cancelledYes. This must be in the contract
price_reportingOne average price or per-fill breakdownYes

Settlement follows the target model in 2.1 (Uphold wallets ↔ LP/DLT accounts). Because Uphold is both the client and the settlement wallet operator, the flow is simpler than the third-party case. Pre-fund or pre-position collateral at the hedge LPs so a liquidation is never blocked by credit lines exactly when markets are moving.

Shortcut Go-live is further out, so don't build a bespoke algo engine. Version 1 = IOC retry ladder + simple time-sliced TWAP inside the existing hedging engine. Once Haruko's OEMS is connected, move the slicing/VWAP logic to Haruko's algos and keep only the "hedge-first, then fill client" orchestration on our side.

2.3 OEMS / prime-broker flows (OneZero on DLT's B2C2 and other accounts)

What it is. Some clients trade through an OEMS such as OneZero directly on DLT's dedicated accounts at B2C2 and other LPs. DLT only records the transactions. It takes no market risk and holds no inventory, and acts as prime broker between the parties.

  • "No risk" is not quite true: DLT is still B2C2's counterparty. If a client doesn't settle, DLT owes B2C2. Make sure each client's OEMS session has pre-trade credit limits no larger than its posted collateral, plus a kill switch.
  • Target settlement: same model as 2.1. Settlement runs directly between Uphold wallets and client wallets, so DLT isn't moving coins itself.
  • Recording: drop-copy from the OEMS/LP into our trade store today, and into Haruko later.

Low priority because demand is low. Don't build anything new. Keep it safe (limits, reconciliation) and move it to the target settlement model in the same project as 2.1.

2.4 Nexo spot: prop until Nexo's MiCAR licence

For now we still have to do proper prop spot trading for Nexo. That responsibility ends when Nexo receives its own MiCAR licence in Q1 2027.

Temporary by design Keep it running safely and invest nothing. Prepare a wind-down checklist now: final settlement, inventory unwind, closing LP lines dedicated to Nexo, and archiving records for retention. That way the hand-back is a non-event. Watch out for the licence date slipping. Have a rolling 3-month extension clause rather than an open-ended commitment.

3Perps: DLT as financial commission broker

The principle. Clean vanilla perp trading only works if DLT acts as a financial commission broker (Finanzkommissionsgeschäft). We buy and sell in our own name for the client's account. That role comes with best execution obligations (MiFID II Art. 27 / §82 WpHG). Our current setup, executing all user orders on one venue, is not sufficient on its own. Discussions with BaFin require us to change the setup.

Shortcut that is legitimate Best execution does not require multiple venues. A firm can use a single venue if its execution policy shows that this venue consistently delivers the best overall result for clients (price, costs, speed, likelihood of execution and settlement). That should be documented with data and reviewed regularly. So the fastest path is: (1) health monitoring + execution-quality data, (2) an execution policy that justifies the pre-selection, (3) add venues gradually. Whether BaFin accepts this in our case is part of the open specification. Confirm with Maxi before relying on it.

Transition rule: we will keep improving the product continuously rather than with a big-bang rebuild, and move step by step to a system that meets all requirements. The EDX implementation stays the execution backbone for now. As soon as it's feasible, perps activity moves to Haruko for a consolidated view. New venue connectivity should go through Haruko where possible, so we don't build venue adapters twice.

4Structured products

What they are. Option-based strategies with defined payout profiles, such as Shark Fins (capped upside participation with a knock-out barrier), Covered Calls (long the asset, short a call for yield), Dual Currency Investments, and similar.

  • Market risk limits for the risk we keep (delta / vega / gamma by underlying), even if they're simple at first.

5ETPs / leveraged Delta-1 certificates (tokenized / securitized perps)

5.1 What the Anduin instruments are

Anduin's first product line is the Unlimited Perpetual Knock-Out Certificate, a securitized perp. It is a security token that reproduces the economics and user experience of a perpetual future inside a regulated, prospectus-based transferable security.

FeatureHow it works
Legal formTransferable security under MiFID II (security token, not a CFD and not an OTC derivative). Issued under an EU base prospectus with per-series Final Terms. Issuer: an offshore SPV (Anduin Capital, Jersey or Cayman TBC). Approval via CSSF (LU) or FMA (LI), with an EEA passport.
UnderlyingsCrypto (BTC, ETH, majors), precious metals (gold, silver), equities and indices, commodities (oil, copper).
Price referenceFor crypto, the perp mark price of a reference venue chosen per series (e.g. Bybit, Binance, BitMEX, Hyperliquid…).
LeverageImplicit. The certificate value = (reference price − financing level) × ratio for long positions, and the reverse for short. The gap between price and financing level sets the leverage, much like choosing leverage on a perp.
Knock-out = liquidationIf the reference price hits the knock-out barrier, the certificate terminates. For the user this is the equivalent of a liquidation. No margin calls and no negative balance: the most a user can lose is what they paid.
FundingPerp funding is passed through by adjusting the financing level, so a holder carries the same cost as a perp position.
HedgingAnduin hedges each certificate 1:1 with perp positions at the reference venue / market maker.
Custody & docsDesigned to be held by a German qualified crypto custodian. PRIIPs KID per series (SRI typically 6–7).

Because the certificate's value is a linear function of the underlying (Delta-1), risk management is straightforward. For DLT, the exposure is just the number of certificates held × delta, much like spot inventory.

5.2 Three roles DLT can play

Role (i)
Prop trader / market maker
  • DLT quotes its own prices (spread and/or fee) and runs its own book based on the issuer's book.
  • Hedging is decoupled from client execution. DLT fills the client at the price implied by its hedge venues, then sends the hedge order to Anduin.
  • Risk = units held on the book, using the existing spot approach.
  • Anduin is the only LP (as issuer), so DLT must run a fair market pricing assessment: build its own theoretical certificate price from the reference market (e.g. Bybit mark), compare it with the Anduin quote and the client price, and store every comparison in our database.

Revenue: highest. Risk: small residual delta / timing risk.

Role (ii)
Back-to-back
  • Like prop, but DLT hedges first with Anduin and only then fills the client, so there's no market risk.
  • This would normally bring best-ex requirements. Because Anduin is the only available hedging option, a fair market pricing assessment may be enough. This has to be confirmed with Compliance.

Revenue: spread/fee. Risk: none (except failed hedges).

Role (iii)
Investment / contract broker
  • DLT is not part of the transaction. The end client deals directly with the issuer (Anduin).
  • For German clients, DLT doesn't have to withhold capital gains tax (Kapitalertragsteuer). This was the plan for Nexo from the start.
  • DLT only needs to consume the transaction data between client and Anduin, for reporting and oversight.

Revenue: lowest (commission). Risk: none.

Watch-out: conflicts of interest With Anduin as the only LP and possibly a related party, the fair-price assessment is the core control that protects clients, so it has to be systematic, timestamped and auditable. The minimum record per trade: reference mark price, theoretical certificate value, Anduin quote, client price, spread/fee and timestamps. The conflicts-of-interest policy should name the DLT–Anduin relationship explicitly.

  • User reports: trade confirmations, periodic statements, costs & charges (ex-ante and ex-post).
  • Platform-wide overview for the trading teams: every certificate trade across all partners, positions per partner, knock-out events and funding adjustments.
  • Fair-price engine + store (roles i and ii).
  • Hedge connector to Anduin (roles i and ii). It reuses the partner API work.

Once Haruko is fully in place, all trading and risk for ETPs goes into Haruko for one complete view. Build the fair-price store and the trade store so they can be exported to Haruko from day one.

6Other tokenized securities (xStocks and similar)

What they are. Delta-1 tokenized securities with no leverage. Each token tracks one underlying share or ETF one-to-one (e.g. xStocks: tracker certificates issued by a Jersey SPV under an EU base prospectus, backed by the underlying shares held with a custodian). Other examples of the same pattern: Backed bTokens, Ondo Global Markets tokens.

Recommended role
Prop (not commission broker)

Like ETPs, DLT could act as prop or as commission broker (FiKo). It should be prop. The reasons:

  • Unlike ETPs, several LPs exist: issuer primary mint/redeem (for authorised participants), CEX listings, OTC desks and on-chain liquidity. So DLT can source competitively without a single-LP problem.
  • Commission broker would mean full best-ex across all these venues. Prop with a documented fair-price check is lighter and earns the spread.
  • No leverage and Delta-1 make it simpler than ETPs. Risk = units × price, using the spot approach.
What makes it different from crypto spot
Specific risks
  • Market hours gap: tokens trade 24/7 but the underlying stock doesn't. On weekends and overnight, the hedge price is stale, so we need wider spreads, smaller limits or a "reference market closed" mode.
  • Corporate actions: dividends (paid out or reinvested in the token?), splits, mergers. Each issuer handles them differently, and the book has to follow.
  • Issuer / custody risk: the token is only as good as the issuer's backing. Do due diligence on each issuer.
  • Chain / token standard: xStocks run on several chains. Decide which ones we support.

Regulatory and operational checklist

  • Custody licence: whether our crypto custody licence covers these tokens, or whether the safekeeping-of-securities licence (Depotgeschäft), expected Q3 2027, is needed, depends on how each token is classified legally. This is on the onsite agenda (§11).
  • PRIIPs KID: tracker certificates are packaged products. The issuer's KID must be available and delivered before the trade.
  • Transaction reporting (MiFIR Art. 26): check whether the instrument is admitted to trading on an EU venue. If so, transaction reports are due.
  • Tax: for German clients, if DLT holds custody and pays out, withholding obligations apply. The investment-broker route (as with ETPs) avoids this.
  • Fair-price check: token price vs underlying (primary exchange price × ratio, FX), stored per trade. It's the same store as for ETPs, so we only build it once.
  • Haruko: book, risk and exposure in Haruko like every other MiFID product.
Shortcut Start with a short list of liquid US large caps and ETFs from one issuer on one chain, quoted only during US market hours (or with a wide fixed spread outside them). That avoids most of the weekend-gap and corporate-action complexity in v1. Widen the scope once Haruko and the custody licence are in place.

7Haruko: the target operating system

What it is. Haruko is an OEMS and PMS: venue connectivity, order and execution management, risk management, and an overall view of exposure across our systems.

The rule
All activity on MiFID products, whatever the product, is monitored and carried out through Haruko. Haruko has a full view of DLT's risk, exposure and trading activity and becomes the operating system for DLT as a whole.

11Onsite agenda: to be discussed in person

These topics are deliberately not settled in writing. They need a face-to-face discussion at the next onsite. The notes below are starting points only.

  1. Local server setup / how partners can distribute MiFID products. Partners have three options:
    • (a) being appointed as a tied agent by DLT (DLT is liable for the partner and must supervise it);
    • (b) being appointed as a tied agent by a third party (another liability umbrella carries the partner);
    • (c) deploying their UI on a DLT-controlled server, so that DLT can argue it is only a "brand partnership" and the service is provided by DLT.
    For each option we need to discuss the trade-offs: liability, supervision effort, time to market and how robust it is with BaFin.
  2. Best execution: how far it reaches across perps, ETPs and tokenized securities; single-venue justification.
  3. Prop: where DLT wants to take principal risk and with what limits.
  4. Investment broker: when DLT steps out of the flow (German clients, tax) and what it still has to record and report.
  5. How Nexo does spot trading for securities: their current setup and how it fits with our roles.
  6. Spot stock trading and the safekeeping-of-securities licence (Depotgeschäft) DLT needs, expected in Q3 2027.
