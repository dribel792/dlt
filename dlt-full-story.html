DLT Finance
1What does it do?

Not spot with leverage — a futures contract without an expiry date. You put down €100 and control €5,000 of exposure, and there is never a contract to roll.

A small payment passed between buyers and sellers keeps it glued to the real price. No settlement calendar, no rolls — that one simplification is why perps beat traditional futures for retail.

2Why is it interesting?
Necessity — not a differentiator

Perps are the entry point for our sales. They are the first ask in every crypto exchange conversation — not offering them does not cost us a product line, it costs us the client and the spot business they bring.

It is also the venue where we hedge everything else we sell — one liquidity relationship, several revenue lines.

Why they cannot build it themselves
  • You cannot quote what you cannot hedge. A weekend price with no hedge venue is the broker betting against its own clients. No risk committee signs that off.

Our position in this trade

Why the perp is the shape that won
  • No expiry, no roll — the funding rate does the work. The contract self-anchors to spot through a payment between longs and shorts. A retail user never has to understand contango or a settlement calendar. That single simplification is why perps beat listed futures for retail.
  • One contract shape, every asset class. The same engine that prices a BTC perp prices a gold perp or an NVDA perp. Marginal cost of a new market is close to zero.
  • Crypto exchanges only ever want perps. Across every conversation we have, the ask is the same product — because perps are where their volume, their financing and their P&L sit. Spot is what they must offer; perps are what they earn on.
  • The perp is also our hedge venue. The instrument we sell is the one we use to lay off risk on securitised perps and 24/7 CFDs. One liquidity relationship, three revenue lines.
Strategic point

Perps are not a product line. They are the top of the leverage funnel. Whoever owns the perp owns the customer for everything above it.

For every €1 of spot our clients trade, roughly €5 of perp notional is available on the same underlying — at a higher take rate, with financing on top. Perps do not cannibalise spot; they sit five times on top of it.

How we have to offer it — and the open question

We act as agent. On a plain perp we route and clear the client order against the venue and take a commission. The client faces the venue, not us, and the flow passes straight through — which caps what we can earn on it.

The regulatory future is genuinely uncertain. A perp sold to an EU retail client can be re-characterised as a CFD, which would pull in MiFID leverage caps, negative-balance protection and marketing restrictions. No regulator has settled this.

Which is exactly why the securitised version exists. Same economics, inside a wrapper whose treatment is not in question — issued by a partner, and brokered by us as the client’s counterparty rather than as a pass-through agent.

What this means for the strategy
  • Leverage is the P&L. Fund it first. Securitised perps with Nexo in Q4 is still the single highest-value item on the roadmap.

  • Structured products punch above their volume. 12% of revenue from ~7% of users, at the fattest margin on the shelf — and they lock the deposit for 1–3 months.
  • Stop investing to win Delta-1. 100% of users, 8% of revenue, 200+ competitors. Defend it as acquisition; do not fund it as a product line.
The ask

Endorse the sequencing: securitised perps → 24/7 RWA markets (commodities & metals first) → structured shelf on new underlyings. Everything else is downstream of the MiFID licence and risk engine we already have — we broker and distribute; partner issuers supply the wrapper.
