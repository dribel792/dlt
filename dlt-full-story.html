Perps — the price of entry

DLT Finance
1What does it do?

Spot, but you put down €100 and control €5,000 of exposure — and there is no expiry date to manage.

A perp stays glued to the real price through a small payment passed between buyers and sellers. The user never has to roll a contract or understand a settlement calendar. That one simplification is why perps beat traditional futures for retail.

2Why is it interesting?
Necessity — not a differentiator

Everyone has them. Not offering perps does not cost us a product line — it costs us the client, who takes their spot business with them.

For every €1 of spot our clients trade, roughly €5 of perp notional trades on the same asset, at a higher take rate with financing on top. It is also the venue where we hedge everything else we sell — one liquidity relationship, three revenue lines.

DLT Finance

The same leveraged bet as a perp — but packaged as a security with an ISIN, so it sits in a normal brokerage account next to a share. And the client can never lose more than they put in.

Turbos, knock-out certificates, mini futures and securitised perps all live here. Same machinery, different barriers. Up to 30× leverage, no margin calls, no negative balances.

2Why is it interesting?

It also removes every blocker on their side: a distributor with no derivatives licence, no margin engine and no liquidation policy can list it like any other security. High leverage is the strongest retail demand signal in this market, and this is the only compliant way most venues can reach it.

Structured products — first, what they actually are

DLT Finance
The whole category in four sentences

A structured product is a package. Most of the money goes somewhere safe that earns interest. A small slice buys an option. The safe part guarantees you get your money back; the option part is the upside. Change the mix and you change the payoff — that is the entire idea.

~90% kept safe, earning interestthis is what guarantees the capital comes back
~10%buys upside

1What does it do?example: shark fin

Your money back, guaranteed. Plus up to 8% if BTC stays inside a range. If it breaks out you still get your money back — you just lose the bonus.

One recipe among many — covered calls, protected notes and dual-currency are the same machinery with a different mix.

Three outcomes, in plain language
Best
BTC drifts up and stays inside the rangeCapital back, plus the full bonus. This is the scenario the product is designed for.
Flat / down
BTC goes nowhere, or fallsCapital back, plus base interest. The client is no worse off than holding cash — and better off than holding BTC.
Breakout
BTC rips through the top of the rangeCapital back, plus base interest. The client gives up the bonus — the one real cost, and it is an opportunity cost, not a loss.
Why this is the example we use

It is the easiest structured product to explain to someone who has only ever held spot: you cannot lose your money, and here is what you give up in exchange. Every other recipe on the shelf is a variation on that trade-off.

What the four products add up to
  • Perps are the entry ticket. 12% of revenue, and we run them at agency margin — but without them we do not get the conversation. Offer them, do not fund them as a growth line.

Why they cannot build it themselves
  • You cannot quote what you cannot hedge. A weekend price with no hedge venue is the broker betting against its own clients. No risk committee signs that off.
  • DeFi now supplies both missing legs. RWA perps are a real hedge venue — continuously funded and marked. Tokenised equity spot gives the reference price when the primary exchange is dark. Stablecoins let margin move on a Sunday.
  • But a bank cannot hold a perp on an offshore DEX. It needs the exposure to arrive as an ISIN-bearing, MiFID-eligible instrument. That transformation is the product we sell.
Our position in this trade

Liquidity sits in crypto venues. Demand sits in regulated distribution. Neither can touch the other directly. DLT sits in the middle and performs four transformations — venue, format, currency and legal wrapper — and charges for all four. The wrapper itself is issued by a partner; we arrange and broker it.

  • No expiry, no roll — the funding rate does the work. The contract self-anchors to spot through a payment between longs and shorts. A retail user never has to understand contango or a settlement calendar. That single simplification is why perps beat listed futures for retail.
  • One contract shape, every asset class. The same engine that prices a BTC perp prices a gold perp or an NVDA perp. Marginal cost of a new market is close to zero.
  • Crypto exchanges only ever want perps. Across every conversation we have, the ask is the same product — because perps are where their volume, their financing and their P&L sit. Spot is what they must offer; perps are what they earn on.
  • The perp is also our hedge venue. The instrument we sell is the one we use to lay off risk on securitised perps and 24/7 CFDs. One liquidity relationship, three revenue lines.
Strategic point

Perps are not a product line. They are the top of the leverage funnel. Whoever owns the perp owns the customer for everything above it.

For every €1 of spot our clients trade, roughly €5 of perp notional is available on the same underlying — at a higher take rate, with financing on top. Perps do not cannibalise spot; they sit five times on top of it.

How we have to offer it — and the open question

We act as agent. On a plain perp we route and clear the client order against the venue and take a commission. The client faces the venue, not us, and the flow passes straight through — which caps what we can earn on it.

The regulatory future is genuinely uncertain. A perp sold to an EU retail client can be re-characterised as a CFD, which would pull in MiFID leverage caps, negative-balance protection and marketing restrictions. No regulator has settled this.

Which is exactly why the securitised version exists. Same economics, inside a wrapper whose treatment is not in question — issued by a partner, and brokered by us as the client’s counterparty rather than as a pass-through agent.

What this means for the strategy
  • Leverage is the P&L. Fund it first. Securitised perps with Nexo in Q4 is still the single highest-value item on the roadmap.
  • 24/7 RWA markets are the growth line. CFD leverage plus delta-1 on equities, metals and commodities — 20% of revenue on a fixed cost base, and the only thing TradFi cannot copy.
  • Structured products punch above their volume. 12% of revenue from ~7% of users, at the fattest margin on the shelf — and they lock the deposit for 1–3 months.
  • Stop investing to win Delta-1. 100% of users, 8% of revenue, 200+ competitors. Defend it as acquisition; do not fund it as a product line.
The ask

Endorse the sequencing: securitised perps → 24/7 RWA markets (commodities & metals first) → structured shelf on new underlyings. Everything else is downstream of the MiFID licence and risk engine we already have — we broker and distribute; partner issuers supply the wrapper.
