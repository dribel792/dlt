BingX × DLT × Anduin — Integration Proposal

Fast-Path Execution: Latency-Optimized Flow for Securitized Perps on BingX

October 2026 · Working draft v0.1 — supplements the Technical Integration & Flow of Funds doc (v0.3); addresses the BingX MM latency concern

The BingX MM's concern with the baseline chain (User → BingX → DLT → Anduin → MM, and the same hops back for quotes) is that round-trip latency in both directions forces either wider spreads, larger slippage buffers, or worse MM PnL. This document restructures the flow so that no latency-critical hop runs through DLT, while the regulatory order chain remains exactly the baseline chain. Two changes: (1) the quote stream goes MM → Anduin → BingX directly, with Anduin as a thin validating proxy; (2) on order entry, BingX fires a parallel hedge trigger straight to Anduin so the MM has already executed by the time the client order arrives through the regulatory chain — the chain then plays back execution details instead of waiting for a fresh price.

1 · The principle — two tracks per order

TrackWhat runs on itLatency requirement
Fast path operational Quote streaming (MM → Anduin → BingX) and the hedge trigger on order entry (BingX → Anduin → MM). Carries no client order and no securities trade — it is the issuer's own hedging machinery, fed by an early signal from the distribution frontend. Milliseconds — co-located, WS/FIX, no intermediate consumer.
Regulatory path legal The client order and the securities trade: BingX (tied agent) RTO → DLT (executing broker) STP/back-to-back → Anduin (issuer, counterparty of the cert trade). Booking, fee collection, T+0 reporting — unchanged from the baseline doc. Not latency-critical anymore — the price is already locked by the fast path when the order arrives; this track confirms and books.

Why this kills the latency problem

In the baseline, the MM quotes a price at T0 and fills the hedge at T0 + (4 order hops) — the gap is MM risk, priced into the spread. In the fast path, the time between the user clicking and the MM filling is two co-located hops (BingX → Anduin → MM, with Anduin proxying the MM's own execution API). The MM prices against a gap of milliseconds, not of a full multi-party round trip — so it can quote tighter spreads with less slippage reserve. The regulatory chain stops being a pricing constraint entirely.

2 · Quoting flow — DLT out of the streaming path

The MM streams the cert books (or perp books, per Flow 1/Flow 2 of the baseline doc) to Anduin. Anduin validates against its published levels, writes every tick to its insert-only quote archive, and proxies the stream directly to BingX — which renders it to the user as the reference price. DLT receives a non-latency-critical mirror of the same stream for surveillance and reconciliation; it is no longer a hop in the display chain.

3 · Execution flow — hedge trigger first, order chain plays back

On order entry BingX does two things simultaneously: it fires the hedge trigger to Anduin (fast path) and transmits the client order to DLT (regulatory path). Both carry the same client_order_ref. Anduin forwards the hedge order to the MM immediately — by the time the RTO'd client order arrives via DLT, the MM fill already exists, and Anduin matches it by reference to fix the cert execution price. The regulatory chain then plays the execution details back: Anduin → DLT (books T+0, collects fee) → BingX → user.

Timing summary

  1. t=0 — user clicks. BingX stamps client_order_ref.
  2. t+ms — hedge trigger reaches the MM via Anduin's proxy; MM executes against the price it is itself streaming. Slippage window ≈ co-located round trip.
  3. t+X — client order arrives at Anduin via DLT. X is irrelevant to pricing: the fill is locked, X only delays the booking confirmation.
  4. t+X+ε — trade booked T+0 at DLT, execution details back to the user. The user-visible confirmation latency is the regulatory chain; the price the user gets is from t+ms.

4 · Exception handling — when the two tracks disagree

The fast path executes a hedge before the client order is legally confirmed. The window is short (one RTO hop + one STP hop) but must be handled deterministically:

CaseHandling
Client order never arrives (timeout Tmatch, e.g. 2–5 s) Anduin unwinds the hedge at the MM. Unwind cost allocation per the MM agreement (expected ≈ 0 in a co-located setup; the MM is both fill and unwind counterparty). Event archived with both legs.
Order rejected by DLT (balance, POSOF, KYC, sanctions) Same as above — DLT's reject reaches Anduin via the STP leg or the mirror channel; Anduin unwinds and archives reject reason + unwind fill.
Quantity mismatch (partial user balance → DLT trims the order) Anduin trims the hedge to the confirmed quantity; excess unwound as above. Cert execution price derived from the retained portion of the fill (FIFO on the fill slices).
KO fires between fill and match The locked fill timestamp decides: trade timestamped before the KO observation → trade is valid and settles; after → order rejected, hedge unwound. Single authoritative clock: Anduin's lifecycle daemon.

Residual risk sits with Anduin, bounded and auditable

Between MM fill and DLT confirmation Anduin holds a hedge without a confirmed client trade — the same exposure class as Flow 1's quote-to-hedge gap in the baseline doc, but shorter and fully referenced (client_order_ref on both legs). Every trigger, fill, match, trim and unwind lands in the insert-only archive, so the full lifecycle of every order is reconstructible per reference — that is also the audit answer to "did any client trade execute without its regulatory leg": the match table is complete by construction.

5 · Fees — economics unchanged, applied without a latency hop

In the baseline, DLT adds its spread/fee while relaying the stream. With DLT out of the streaming path, the fee stays DLT's but is applied without a hop: the DLT fee is a static parameter (bps schedule agreed with DLT), rendered by BingX in the displayed price or shown as an explicit fee line on the ticket. Collection is unchanged: DLT collects at the T+0 booking and credits the agreed share to the BingX Tied Agent Entity. Anduin's economics remain in the issuer spread inside the MM quote. No party's economics move; only the point of application does.

6 · What stays regulatorily identical

The legal chain is untouched

  • Order chain: every client order runs BingX (tied agent of DLT, RTO) → DLT (executing broker, STP/back-to-back) → Anduin (issuer, counterparty). The fast path carries no client order — it is Anduin's own hedging, triggered early.
  • Booking & reporting: DLT books every securities trade T+0 and remains the regulatory reporting counterparty; POSOF/KYC gates sit where they sat.
  • Best execution: unchanged — no other venue lists the tokens; back-to-back against the issuer at the formula price satisfies best ex. The locked MM fill is the formula input, identically to the baseline.
  • Reference price label: the streamed price is pre-trade indicative; the binding price is the one confirmed through the regulatory chain — which equals the locked fill. No user is dealt a price outside the published quote + disclosed fees.
  • Flow of funds: Part B of the baseline doc applies 1:1 — BingX EU custody accounts, mirror-margin at the MM, pledge, T+1 rebalance.

Hosting

Fast path assumes co-location of BingX's order gateway, Anduin's issuer stack and the MM engine in the same datacenter. As in the baseline doc: shared datacenter, but every hop through the respective party's own servers — latency is compressed, the chain of distinct operators is not.
