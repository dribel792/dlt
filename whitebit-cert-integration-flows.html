Securitized Perps on Whitebit: Technical Integration Flow & Flow of Funds

November 2026 · Working draft v0.1 — companion to the Securitized Perps Listing Primer; adapts the KO Certificate Trading Flow (July 2026) to the Whitebit relationship

A0 · What is being sold here — three claims up front

1 · The instrument

What is sold is a securitized perp: legally, an open-ended knock-out certificate — a transferable security under MiFID II, issued off an EU-passported base prospectus with Final Terms and a PRIIPs KID per series, settled as a token held in custody. Economically, one certificate is a perp position: same price formula (price = (mark − strike) × ratio), same 8-hour funding (applied by moving the strike), and the knock-out barrier is the liquidation level.

2 · It mirrors perps 100% — and adds what perps can't offer in the EU

  • 1:1 perp economics. Entry price, funding, PnL and liquidation produce the same numbers a perp produces. KO = liquidation — same level, same outcome. A user who knows perps already knows this product.
  • >30× leverage, legally, to EU retail. As a prospectus-regulated security the product is not caught by the CFD-style leverage caps — leverage well beyond 30× can be offered compliantly in the EU.
  • B-book economics stay with Whitebit. The MM leg is the Whitebit MM or the Whitebit global orderbook — all flow can go back to Whitebit, which keeps the internalization economics of a b-book while the client-facing product is a fully regulated security.

3 · The flows below are vanilla perps flows

Everything the reader sees in Parts A and B — quote streaming, order entry, execution, funding, liquidation, margin and PnL rebalancing — is exactly the flow of vanilla perps trading. The securities layer (prospectus, series docs, token settlement, the broker hop) runs in the back office and changes nothing about how the product trades or how the screen looks.

This document answers the two questions from the kick-off: what does the integration look like on a technical level — who does what in this relationship — and what does the flow of funds look like. Part A presents the two options for pricing-data provision and execution (Flow 1: perp books in, the Issuer builds the cert book · Flow 2: cert books in, full STP). Part B presents the flow of funds with Whitebit's MiCAR entity as custodian. Names and payload shapes are indicative; the goal is to agree the shape.

Issuer Issuer of record. Owns the golden record per series, generates and publishes Final Terms + PRIIPs KID, mints the tokens, is the counterparty of every client cert trade, runs the 8h funding sync (strike/barrier), monitors and declares knock-outs, computes residual values. Flow 2: consumes cert books from the MM and STPs every order to the MM. Flat market risk in both. Registry + doc-gen, token contracts, golden record DB, pricing/booking engine (Flow 1) or STP router (Flow 2), lifecycle daemon (funding + KO), WS/FIX market-data service to DLT, public levels/docs endpoints, append-only archive.
MM leg (Whitebit MM / Global Orderbook) Liquidity to the Issuer. Flow 1: streams perp books (WS/FIX) and takes the Issuer's perp hedge trades — this can be the Whitebit MM or directly the Whitebit global orderbook. Flow 2: streams cert books incl. the underlying markets and takes the STP'd cert orders directly — this requires the Whitebit MM (a desk that prices and deals the security bilaterally; an orderbook cannot stream cert books). Manages the resulting market risk at its own discretion. Either way the liquidity leg is Whitebit-side, so the flow economics stay inside Whitebit — the internalization story from the Listing Primer. Pricing engine (perps or certs) / matching engine, WS/FIX market-data + execution API towards the Issuer, own hedging infrastructure, margin account management (see Part B).
Whitebit MiCAR entity (custodian) Whitebit's EU MiCAR entity and the custodian of the assets. User, DLT, the Issuer and (in Option 1) the Whitebit Tied Agent Entity all hold accounts at the Whitebit MiCAR entity. All client-side money and token movements are book entries between these accounts; assets never leave the custody perimeter. Account/booking API, "leverage trading" assignment flags, statements, rebalancing files.

Distribution setup — two options

  • Option 1 — Tied agent. DLT appoints a Whitebit entity as its tied agent. Whitebit operates the UI under that appointment and RTOs / STPs orders to DLT; the Whitebit Tied Agent Entity receives the agreed share of the trading fee from DLT.
  • Option 2 — DLT-hosted local server. Whitebit's derivatives-trading UI is deployed on a local server owned and operated by DLT. Clients interact directly with DLT's system; Whitebit transmits no orders, so no tied-agent appointment is required. Whitebit's role is technology licensor (UI), liquidity (MM leg) and custodian (MiCAR entity); its remuneration shifts from a tied-agent fee share to a platform / licence fee.

Everything downstream of order entry — the broker hop, the Issuer leg, the MM leg and the flow of funds in Part B — is identical in both options.

A2 · Pricing & execution — two options

Both options use the same order chain (Whitebit UI → DLT → Issuer) and the same fee logic (DLT adds and collects). They differ in what the MM leg streams and therefore in who prices the certificate and who carries which risk.

Flow 1 — perp books in Issuer pricesFlow 2 — cert books in MM prices
Market data in Perp books of the reference markets, WS/FIX from the Whitebit MM or the global orderbook to the Issuer. Cert books (incl. underlying markets), WS/FIX from the Whitebit MM to the Issuer.
Cert book construction The Issuer derives the cert book from the perp book via the Final Terms formula on current levels. The MM constructs the cert book, keyed to the Issuer's published levels; the Issuer validates and passes through.
Execution leg to the MM Perp hedge trade per client fill; the MM leg confirms the perp execution price; the Issuer derives and confirms the cert execution price to DLT. Cert order STP'd to the MM; the MM confirms the cert execution price directly.
Issuer's residual risk Flat post-hedge; carries the quote-to-hedge latency/slippage gap. Fully riskless — instrument-level back-to-back per fill.
MM requirements Any perp liquidity source works — no securities knowledge needed. MM must price and deal the security itself (bilateral professional-counterparty dealing under the MM agreement).
Who can be the MM leg Whitebit MM or the Whitebit global orderbook directly. Whitebit MM.

Hosting

All of this activity can be hosted in the same datacenter for latency optimization — but every hop must run through the respective party's own servers (the order gateway serving the UI, DLT's broker stack, the Issuer's stack, the MM's engine). Co-location compresses latency; it does not collapse the chain into one operator — each entity demonstrably operates its own system. Option 2's local server is exactly this principle applied to the frontend: the order-entry system runs on DLT-owned infrastructure, with Whitebit as the technology provider.

B · Flow of funds — Whitebit MiCAR entity as custodian

Whitebit is the MiCAR entity in this relationship: its MiCAR-licensed entity is the custodian of the assets. User, DLT, the Issuer and (in Option 1) the Whitebit Tied Agent Entity each hold an account at the Whitebit MiCAR entity. Client-side cash and token movements are book entries between these accounts. The margin relationship towards the MM leg (Whitebit MM / global orderbook) runs alongside it and is trued up by a periodic rebalancing (T+1 as the working example).

B2 · Purchase (worked example: 10 certs à 700 USDC, fee 0.06%)

Sequence

  1. User buys 10 certs via the UI; the order runs the chain of Part A (order entry → DLT STP/prop → Issuer → MM leg).
  2. Cash leg: at the time of purchase the user's funds are credited to DLT including the fee (7,000 + 4.20 USDC), then credited onward to the Issuer without the fee (7,000 USDC). T+0 book entries at the Whitebit MiCAR entity.
  3. Fee split: DLT retains its share and credits the agreed part of the fee to Whitebit's account (tied-agent remuneration in Option 1, platform / licence fee in Option 2).
  4. Security leg (DvP): 10 certs move Issuer Account → User Account.
  5. Pledge: the Issuer pledges the received funds to Whitebit — this is the collateral backing the margin capacity Whitebit mirrored on the MM leg at assignment (see B1 setup; utilized portion).
  6. MM leg: the Issuer's hedge (Flow 1) or STP'd cert order (Flow 2) executes on the Whitebit MM / global orderbook against that margin.

B3 · T+1 rebalancing of realized PnL

Sequence (T+1 as the working example — cadence is a parameter)

  1. Over the day, client sells / knock-outs realize PnL on the Issuer's positions at the Whitebit MM / global orderbook.
  2. Positive realized PnL on the MM leg is moved back to custody (MM leg → Issuer Account @ Whitebit MiCAR) — from there it funds client sale proceeds / KO residual payouts.
  3. Negative realized PnL is topped up in the other direction (Issuer Account @ Whitebit MiCAR → MM leg), keeping the margin account whole.
  4. The mirrored margin capacity is re-trued to the current sum of user leverage-trading assignments; the Issuer's pledge is re-trued to utilized positions.
