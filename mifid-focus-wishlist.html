Part A — Offload spot trading & settlement to Uphold

The principle

For B2B clients, DLT keeps the client-facing contract (our brand, our endpoints, our SLAs) but stops being the principal and operator of spot execution, settlement, and the associated risk. Our endpoints stay the integration surface clients code against; behind them, spot order flow, custody, settlement, and counterparty/credit risk route straight into Uphold's systems.

In short: we keep the API, Uphold keeps the balance sheet and the back office for spot.

What "point our endpoints directly to Uphold" means in practice

  1. Endpoint pass-through, not re-implementation. Our spot endpoints (place order, fills, balances, settlement status) become a thin routing/adapter layer: same request/response schema for the client, but the call is translated and forwarded to the equivalent Uphold endpoint. Clients see no change. We delete the spot engine underneath over time.
  2. Settlement moves onto Uphold's rails. Spot trades settle in Uphold's ledger and custody. DLT no longer holds spot inventory, fronts settlement, or carries the spot float. Spot client balances are Uphold balances, surfaced through our API.
  3. Risk moves with it. Counterparty, credit, and settlement risk on spot become Uphold's risk. Our risk engine stops modelling spot exposure entirely and focuses only on the internal derivatives/structured books (Team 2). This is the single biggest reduction in our risk surface.

What we must nail down with Uphold (open items)

  • Regulatory boundary / who is the executing entity. Is DLT an agent/introducer routing to Uphold (Uphold is principal), or white-label where DLT stays the regulated face? Determines licensing, best-execution obligations, and who owns the client of record. First decision — everything hangs off it.
  • Liability & best execution. If our endpoint forwards to Uphold and Uphold gets a bad fill, who is liable to the client? Allocate clearly in the integration agreement.
  • Latency & SLA. A pass-through adds a hop. Fine for spot, but we need contractual SLAs from Uphold so our client-facing SLA is backable.
  • Data & reporting. We still need read access to spot fills/settlement data to give clients a unified view. Define the feed Uphold gives back.
  • Fee/economics split. Uphold takes spot execution/settlement economics; DLT's margin comes from the MiFID products and from being the integration/distribution layer. Model this so offloading spot doesn't quietly hand away revenue we can't replace.
  • Failover. If Uphold's spot endpoint is down, our spot is down. Decide whether that's acceptable or whether we keep a minimal fallback.

Why this is the right move

  • Removes the most duplication, fastest. Both run a spot execution + post-trade stack. Killing ours frees every engineer maintaining spot.
  • Shrinks our risk + capital footprint. No spot inventory, no settlement float, no spot counterparty risk on our books.
  • Lets us be MiFID-pure. Engineering, risk, compliance, and product all point at one box.

Part B — Team setup to own the MiFID box

Shape: 3–4 product/tech teams, each 4–5 engineers + dedicated QA + Product Manager, plus a dedicated legal capacity (internal + external) mandated to turn around PRIIPs KIDs and product approvals fast.

Why dedicated legal: the rate-limiter on shipping a new structured product (a new BRC, a new principal-protected note, a new tokenised underlying) is not the code — it's the PRIIPs KID, term sheet, and product governance. Shared, queued legal kills product velocity. We give it dedicated lanes.

Team 1 — Back Office / Post-Trade & Reporting

  • Owns: post-trade lifecycle for derivatives & structured products — confirmations, clearing/settlement of MiFID instruments, lifecycle events (corporate actions, coupon/barrier events, expiry/exercise), transaction reporting (MiFIR/EMIR), client statements.
  • Why a team: this is half our moat. Spot post-trade goes to Uphold; derivatives/structured post-trade stays here and is hard.

Team 2 — Risk

  • Owns: the risk engine for the internal books — margin (initial/variation), real-time exposure, P&L, scenario/stress, limits, liquidation, and risk modelling for structured payoffs (barriers, knock-ins/outs, downside protection).
  • Scope tightened by Part A: spot risk goes to Uphold, so this team models only the MiFID/internal books — deeper, not broader. Handles the netting/crossing of internal flow.

Team 3 — API / Platform

  • Owns: the MiFID client-facing API surface — the derivatives & structured-product endpoints clients integrate against. Auth, rate limits, versioning, SDKs, sandbox, docs, and the reporting feed for our MiFID instruments.
  • Why central: owning the API is the MiFID strategy. We are the integration and distribution layer for derivatives and structured products — the API is the product clients build on, and whoever owns it owns the relationship. This is not a spot function; it is the front door to everything in the MiFID box.

Team 4 — New Products (Financial Engineering + Legal)

  • Owns: designing and launching new MiFID products — financial engineers structuring payoffs (notes, certificates, options strategies, new underlyings incl. prediction markets), working hand-in-glove with dedicated legal to produce the PRIIPs KID, term sheet, and product-governance sign-off per launch.
  • Mixed by design: financial engineers + quants + internal legal counsel + a PM, with external legal on retainer for jurisdiction-specific and novel-product questions.
  • Mandate: a short, repeatable launch cycle. This team turns "underlyings are open-ended" into shipped product.
